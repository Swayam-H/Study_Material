Thermodynamics in one pass (Atkins 1A to 3B)

15 min read

Sources: slides thermo.tex (L1-2 plus extras), thermo-l3.tex, thermo-l4.tex; tutorial problem set; Atkins 11e (Atkins, de Paula, Keeler). Page numbers are the book's printed pages. Study order and homework: 10a-quiz2-plan.md. Anything marked [beyond slides] is Atkins only. The third law, free energies and Maxwell relations (Atkins 3C–3E) are on the Free energy and phases page of the next chapter.

Sign convention (get this right first)

ΔU=Q+W,W=−∫Pext​dV​(W=work done ON the system; expansion gives W<0)​
  • The tutorial slides ("First law and heat capacities") say W>0 = work done BY the gas. If a question says "work done by", flip the sign.
  • Engines (L3): QH​, QC​, W are magnitudes, with W=QH​−QC​.
  • In Clausius-inequality formulas (L4) heats are signed, with Q>0 meaning heat in.

1. State, temperature, perfect gas (Atkins 1A, p.4-10)

  • System types: open (matter and energy cross), closed (energy only), isolated (neither).
  • Intensive: P, T, density. Extensive: V, n, U, S. State functions: U,S,H,P,V,T. Path functions: Q, W.
  • Zeroth law: if A and B are each in thermal equilibrium with C, then A and B are in equilibrium. This is why a thermometer works. A metal bench feels colder because it conducts heat away faster; both are at the same T.
  • Constant-volume gas thermometer: P∝T, so T=T3​(P/P3​) with T3​=273.16 K. All gases extrapolate to P=0 at −273.15 ∘C, which defines absolute zero.
PV=nRT=NkB​T​R=8.314 J K−1mol−1=0.08314 L bar K−1mol−1,kB​=1.381×10−23 J K−1​
  • Dalton's law: ptotal​=∑i​pi​, with pi​=xi​p. Exact only in the perfect-gas limit.
  • Trap: when using R=0.08314, keep V in L and P in bar.

2. Kinetic theory (Atkins 1B, p.11-18)

Assumptions: point-like molecules, no forces except elastic collisions, random motion.

  • One molecule: Δp=2mvx​ per hit, time between hits on the same wall 2L/vx​, so F=mvx2​/L.
  • N molecules and ⟨vx2​⟩=⟨v2⟩/3 give
PV=31​Nm⟨v2⟩=32​N⟨Ek​⟩​ PV=NkB​T ​⟨Ek​⟩=23​kB​T​

The mean kinetic energy depends on T only, not on the mass.

vrms​=M3RT​​,vmp​=M2RT​​,vˉ=πM8RT​​​vmp​<vˉ<vrms​
  • For N2​ at 300 K these are 422, 476 and 517 m/s. M in kg/mol (N2​ = 0.028).
  • Maxwell–Boltzmann:
f(v)=4π(2πRTM​)3/2v2e−Mv2/2RT​

The v2 factor means more ways to have a high speed; the exponential means high energy is rare. Raising T moves the peak right and flattens it.

  • vmp​ derivation: dvd​(v2e−Mv2/2RT)=0 gives v2=2RT/M.
  • Fraction in a narrow window ≈f(v)Δv.
  • Escape of He: vrms​ at 300 K is 1.37 km/s, far below 11.2 km/s. Even so, the high-speed tail lets a small fraction escape, and collisions refill the tail.
  • Equal T means equal ⟨Ek​⟩ (H2​ and O2​ in one box). H2​ is 4× faster because M differs by 16.
  • [beyond slides] Collisions (1B.2): cross-section σ=πd2, vrel​=2​vˉ, collision frequency z=σvrel​N/V=σvrel​p/kB​T, mean free path
λ=zvrel​​=σpkB​T​

3. Real gases (Atkins 1C, p.19-27)

Z=RTPVm​​​

Z=1 is ideal; Z<1 means attraction dominates; Z>1 means repulsion (size) dominates, which happens at high P.

(P+Vm2​a​)(Vm​−b)=RT⟺P=Vm​−bRT​−Vm2​a​​(van der Waals)​
  • b is the excluded volume (repulsion). a/Vm2​ is the pressure reduction from attraction; it goes as (n/V)2 because attraction needs pairs of molecules.
  • Tutorial 4 (5 mol in 1 L, 298 K): ideal 124 bar; N2​ 119 bar (Z=0.96); He 140 bar (Z=1.13).
  • [beyond slides] Critical constants and virial expansion:
Tc​=27Rb8a​,Vc​=3b,pc​=27b2a​,Z=1+Vm​B​+Vm2​C​+⋯

Corresponding states: gases at the same reduced (pr​,Tr​,Vr​) behave alike.

  • The ideal law fails at high P (finite size) and low T (attraction).

4. Work, heat, first law (Atkins 2A.1-2A.3, p.34-42)

  • ∣W∣ is the area under the P–V path, so it is a path function. Q is also a path function. Their sum ΔU is a state function.
  • Quasi-static means a sequence of equilibrium states. Reversible = quasi-static + no friction.
Wrev,iso​=−nRTlnV1​V2​​​,ΔU=0,Q=−W(1 mol, 300 K, 10→20 L:W=−1.73 kJ)​
  • Free expansion (insulated, into vacuum): Q=0, W=0, ΔU=0, so ΔT=0 for an ideal gas.
  • Against a constant external pressure: W=−Pext​ΔV, which is less work than reversible.
  • Heat is energy in transit, not something a body contains. Joule's paddle wheel: 4.18 J = 1 cal.
  • dU is exact and dQ, dW are inexact: ∮dU=0 but ∮dQ=0.
  • Other work: dW=FdL (wire; plus sign), γdA (a film has two surfaces, so dA=2ℓdx), EdZ (cell, W=nFE), EdΠ, μ0​HdM. Generalised: dW=YdX.
  • [beyond slides] Calorimetry (2A.4): a bomb calorimeter works at constant volume, so qV​=ΔU. Calibrate once (Ccal​=q/ΔT), then ΔU=Ccal​ΔT (homework E2C.4).

5. Heat capacity and enthalpy (Atkins 2A.4, 2B, p.42-50)

CV​=(∂T∂U​)V​,CP​=(∂T∂H​)P​​H=U+PV,ΔH=QP​​

(ΔH=QP​ holds at constant P with no extra work.)

CP,m​−CV,m​=R,γ=CV​CP​​​(ideal gas)
Gas (equipartition, 21​kT per quadratic term) CV,m​ CP,m​ γ
monatomic 23​R 25​R 5/3
diatomic (vibration frozen at room T) 25​R 27​R 7/5
  • For an ideal gas ΔU=nCV​ΔT​ for every process, because U depends on T only.
  • Constant-P heating of 1 mol monatomic gas by 100 K: Q=+2.08 kJ, W=−0.83 kJ, ΔU=+1.25 kJ.
  • [beyond slides] Reactions with gases: ΔH=ΔU+Δng​RT​, where Δng​ is the change in moles of gas (2B.1; homework E2B.2, E2C.6). Real heat capacities vary with T: Cp,m​=a+bT+c/T2, so integrate (2B.2; homework Ex 2B.2, E2B.3).

6. Ideal-gas processes (Atkins 2E, p.67-69)

Process W (on gas) Q ΔU
Isochoric 0 nCV​ΔT nCV​ΔT
Isobaric −PΔV=−nRΔT nCP​ΔT nCV​ΔT
Isothermal (rev) −nRTln(V2​/V1​) −W 0

TVγ−1=const,PVγ=const​(reversible adiabatic)
  • Derivation: nCV​dT=−(nRT/V)dV, then integrate using R/CV​=γ−1.
  • The adiabat is steeper than the isotherm because γ>1. Compression heats the gas. Fire syringe: T2​=300⋅100.4=754 K, P2​=25 bar.
  • Same Q=0, different outcomes: free expansion has ΔT=0, while reversible adiabatic expansion cools.
  • Atkins writes the adiabat as Vi​Tic​=Vf​Tfc​ with c=CV,m​/R (2E.2b), and wad​=CV​ΔT (2E.1).

7. Differentials, Joule and Joule-Thomson (Atkins 2D, p.59-66)

dU=CV​dT+πT​dVπT​=(∂V∂U​)T​=T(∂T∂p​)V​−p​(perfect gas 0; vdW a/Vm2​>0)​
  • Joule's experiment saw no ΔT only because the water bath swamped it. Real gases cool slightly on free expansion (N2​ doubling: ΔT≈−0.13 K in the gas alone).
  • Joule–Thomson: steady throttling through a plug, Q=0, so Hi​=Hf​ (isenthalpic).
μJT​=(∂p∂T​)H​​(μ>0:cools; μ<0:warms)μ≈CP,m​1​(RT2a​−b)TI​=Rb2a​​​
  • Molecular picture: when attractions dominate, molecules climb out of potential wells and slow down (cooling); when repulsions dominate, the gas warms. High T favours repulsions. For N2​, TI​≈850 K from vdW (measured 621 K).
  • Data: CO2​ μ=+1.1 K/atm; N2​ +0.27; H2​ −0.03 (TI​∼200 K); He −0.06 (TI​∼40 K). H2​ and He warm when throttled at room T.
  • μT​=(∂H/∂p)T​=−CP​μ. Both πT​ and μT​ are 0 for a perfect gas.
  • Linde refrigerator: compress, throttle, counter-current heat exchange, repeat.
  • Exact versus inexact differentials (2D.1): dU, dH are exact; test Mdx+Ndy with ∂M/∂y=∂N/∂x [beyond slides].
  • [beyond slides] α=V1​(∂T∂V​)p​, κT​=−V1​(∂p∂V​)T​ (2D.2). Perfect gas: α=1/T, κT​=1/p (homework Ex 2D.2, P2D.2).

8. Second law and engines (Atkins 3A.1, 3A.3, p.78-86; slides L3)

  • Over a cycle ΔU=0, so Qnet​=−Wnet​. The work equals the enclosed area (clockwise is an engine).
η=QH​W​=1−QH​QC​​​COPfridge​=WQC​​,COPheat pump​=WQH​​​(COP can exceed 1)​
  • Kelvin–Planck: no cyclic device converts heat from a single reservoir entirely into work. Clausius: no cyclic device moves heat cold → hot with no other effect. A perfect fridge combined with an ordinary engine gives a 100% engine, so the two are equivalent.
  • Irreversibility sources: friction, finite-ΔT heat flow, free expansion, mixing, fast change.
  • Carnot cycle: isothermal expansion at TH​ (QH​=nRTH​ln(V2​/V1​)), adiabatic expansion, isothermal compression at TC​, adiabatic compression. The adiabats force V2​/V1​=V3​/V4​, so
QH​QC​​=TH​TC​​,ηCarnot​=1−TH​TC​​​COPfridge,max​=TH​−TC​TC​​,COPhp,max​=TH​−TC​TH​​​​
  • Carnot's theorem: no engine beats a reversible one, and all reversible engines between the same two reservoirs are equally efficient (proof: run the Carnot engine backwards and use the Clausius statement).
  • Kelvin scale: TC​/TH​≡QC​/QH​ for a reversible engine.
  • Otto: η=1−r1−γ. Fridge door open in a closed kitchen: the room warms, because W ends up as heat.
  • Check an inventor's claim in two steps: first law (W=QH​−QC​), then compare η with 1−TC​/TH​.

9. Entropy (Atkins 3A.2, 3A.3(c), 3B, p.80-91; slides L4)

  • Carnot gives QH​/TH​+QC​/TC​=0 (signed heats), so ∮dQrev​/T=0. In general:
∮TdQ​≤0  (Clausius),dS=TdQrev​​​(1/T is the integrating factor; for any process, invent a reversible path)​
Process ΔS
isothermal ideal gas nRln(Vf​/Vi​)​
heating (C constant) Cln(Tf​/Ti​)​ (CP​ at constant P, CV​ at constant V)
phase change at Ttrs​ ΔHtrs​/Ttrs​​
both V and T change nRln(Vf​/Vi​)+nCV,m​ln(Tf​/Ti​) [beyond slides]
  • Trouton: ΔSvap​≈85 J K−1 mol−1. Water is higher (109) because of hydrogen bonding. Vaporisation ≫ fusion (22 for ice).
  • ΔSgas​ is the same for reversible and free expansion; only ΔSsurr​ and ΔSuniv​ differ (reversible: ΔSuniv​=0; free: ΔSuniv​=+nRln2).
  • Heat flow Q from TH​ to TC​: ΔSuniv​=Q(TC​1​−TH​1​)>0.
ΔSisolated​≥0,ΔSuniv​=ΔSsys​+ΔSsurr​≥0​

A system can lose entropy if the surroundings gain more (freezing water).

S=kB​lnW​,W=n!(N−n)!N!​ (left/right box)
  • Doubling the volume gives Wf​/Wi​=2N, so ΔS=Nkln2=nRln2, matching Clausius. The log makes S additive.
  • For large N the width is ∼1/N​, so "irreversible" means overwhelmingly improbable to reverse.
  • Entropy is not just "disorder": a shuffled deck has no thermodynamic ΔS, and crystallisation can be spontaneous because Ssurr​ rises more.
  • [beyond slides] Composite processes (3B.4): split the change into reversible steps and add. Phase change away from Ttrs​ (supercooled water freezing at −10 ∘C): heat to Ttrs​, transform, cool back.
  • Third law (Atkins 3C, p.92): S→0 as T→0 for a perfect crystal. See Free energy and phases.

10. Thermochemistry (Atkins 2C, p.51-58) [beyond slides, but in the homework]

  • Standard state (∘): pure substance at exactly 1 bar, at the stated temperature (tables use 298.15 K).
  • Physical change: Δsub​H∘=Δfus​H∘+Δvap​H∘ at the same T. Reversing a process flips the sign of ΔH.
  • Hess's law: the standard enthalpy of an overall reaction is the sum of those of the steps it can be split into (homework Ex 2C.1).
Δr​H∘=∑νΔf​H∘(products)−∑νΔf​H∘(reactants)​(Δf​H∘=0 for an element in its reference state)​
Δr​H∘(T2​)=Δr​H∘(T1​)+∫T1​T2​​Δr​Cp∘​dT​(Kirchhoff; constant Δr​Cp∘​:+Δr​Cp∘​(T2​−T1​))​
  • A bomb calorimeter gives Δr​U; convert with Δr​H=Δr​U+Δng​RT (homework E2C.4, E2C.6).
  • DSC: integrating the excess heat capacity Cp,ex​ over the transition gives the transition enthalpy (homework P2C.10).
