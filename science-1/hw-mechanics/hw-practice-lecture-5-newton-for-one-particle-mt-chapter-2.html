HW practice: Lecture 5, Newton for one particle (MT Chapter 2)

11 min read · 9 practice problems

Solutions stay collapsed until you click them.

Marion and Thornton, Classical Dynamics of Particles and Systems (5th ed.). Examples 2.2, 2.4, 2.10 and Problems 2-27, 2-32, 2-42, 2-45, 2-47, 2-53.

Problem 1 [HW practice · MT E2.2]

A block rests on a fixed inclined plane with coefficient of static friction μs​=0.4. At what angle θ will the block start sliding if it is initially at rest?

Solution

Solution. Take x down the plane and y perpendicular to it. Perpendicular to the plane there is no acceleration, so N=mgcosθ. Along the plane the friction force fs​ points up the plane and has maximum value μs​N.

The block stays at rest while mgsinθ≤fmax​=μs​mgcosθ. It just starts to slide when the two are equal:

mgsinθ=μs​mgcosθ ⇒ tanθ=μs​
θ=tan−1(0.4)=22∘​

The mass cancels, so the result does not depend on the block's mass. Measuring this angle is a standard way to determine μs​.

Problem 2 [HW practice · MT E2.4]

Find the displacement and velocity of horizontal motion in a medium in which the retarding force is proportional to the velocity, F=−kmv.

Solution

Solution. The equation of motion along x is

mv˙=−kmv ⇒ vdv​=−kdt ⇒ lnv=−kt+C1​

With v(0)=v0​:

v(t)=v0​e−kt​

Integrate again with x(0)=0:

x(t)=∫0t​v0​e−kt′dt′=kv0​​(1−e−kt)​

As t→∞, x→v0​/k, so the body travels a finite distance although it never quite stops. Eliminating t gives v=v0​−kx: the velocity falls linearly with distance. The time constant is 1/k.

Problem 3 [HW practice · MT E2.10]

A charged particle (charge q, mass m) enters a region of uniform magnetic field B=B0​j^​. Determine its subsequent motion.

Solution

Solution. The force is F=qv×B. With v=(x˙,y˙​,z˙) and B=(0,B0​,0),

v×B=(−z˙B0​, 0, x˙B0​)

so with α=qB0​/m:

x¨=−αz˙,y¨​=0,z¨=αx˙

y: y˙​=y˙​0​ is constant and y=y˙​0​t+y0​: uniform motion along B.

x,z: differentiate the first and substitute the third (and vice versa):

x...​=−αz¨=−α2x˙ ⇒ x˙=Acos(αt+δ),z˙=Asin(αt+δ)

so the velocity component perpendicular to B has constant magnitude A=v⊥​ and rotates at the cyclotron frequency ωc​=α=qB0​/m. Integrating once more gives a circle in the xz plane of radius

R=ωc​v⊥​​=qB0​mv⊥​​​

Combined with the uniform motion along y, the path is a helix of radius R whose axis is parallel to B and whose pitch is 2πy˙​0​/ωc​. The speed is constant because the magnetic force does no work.

Problem 4 [HW practice · MT 2-27]

A rope of total mass 0.4 kg and total length 4 m has 0.6 m of the rope hanging vertically down off a work bench. How much work must be done to place all the rope on the bench?

Solution

Solution. The part on the bench does not move vertically. The hanging part has mass

mh​=(0.4 kg)40.6​=0.06 kg

and its centre of mass is 0.3 m below the bench. Raising it to the bench level raises its potential energy by

W=mh​g20.6​=(0.06)(9.8)(0.3)=0.18 J​

(Equivalently, W=∫00.6​λgydy=21​λg(0.6)2 with λ=0.1 kg/m.) The bench is assumed frictionless, and the rope is pulled slowly so no kinetic energy is left over.

Problem 5 [HW practice · MT 2-32]

Two blocks of unequal masses (2m on an incline of angle θ, m hanging) are connected by a string over a smooth pulley. If the coefficient of kinetic friction is μk​, what angle θ of the incline allows the masses to move at constant speed?

Solution

Solution. Constant speed means zero acceleration for both blocks, so the tension is T=mg from the hanging block. For the block of mass 2m on the incline, the forces along the incline are gravity 2mgsinθ (down the slope), the tension T (up the slope) and kinetic friction μk​N=μk​(2mgcosθ), which opposes the motion.

Block 2m sliding down the incline (the hanging block rises): friction acts up the slope,

2mgsinθ=T+2μk​mgcosθ=mg+2μk​mgcosθ
sinθ−μk​cosθ=21​​ ⇒ θ=tan−1μk​+sin−121+μk2​​1​

For μk​=0 this gives θ=30∘ and increases with μk​.

(If instead the block 2m moves up the incline, friction acts down the slope and sinθ+μk​cosθ=21​, so θ=sin−121+μk2​​1​−tan−1μk​, which is smaller than 30∘.)

Use the substitution μk​=tanφ: sinθ−μk​cosθ=sin(θ−φ)/cosφ, so sin(θ−φ)=21​cosφ.

Problem 6 [HW practice · MT 2-42]

A solid cube of uniform density and side b is in equilibrium on top of a cylinder of radius R. The planes of four sides of the cube are parallel to the axis of the cylinder. The contact is perfectly rough. Under what conditions is the equilibrium stable or not stable?

Solution

Solution. A perfectly rough contact means the cube rolls without slipping on the cylinder. Tilt the cube by a small angle θ. The contact point moves along the cylinder by arc length Rθ, the same distance along the cube's bottom face. Measured from the cylinder axis, the cube's centre of mass (initially directly above the contact point, at height R+b/2) is then at height

h(θ)=(R+2b​)cosθ+Rθsinθ

(the first term is the rotated line from axis to the centre of mass; the second is the shift because the contact point moves sideways by Rθ relative to the cube's centre).

Expand for small θ:

h≈(R+2b​)(1−2θ2​)+Rθ2=R+2b​+θ2(2R​−4b​)

The potential energy U=Mgh has a minimum at θ=0 (stable) if the coefficient of θ2 is positive:

R>2b​: stable;R<2b​: unstable;R=2b​: neutral (needs the θ4 term)​

The cube is stable if the cylinder's radius of curvature is larger than half the cube's side, i.e. if the centre of mass lies below the centre of curvature of the "rocking" path.

Problem 7 [HW practice · MT 2-45]

Describe how to determine whether an equilibrium is stable or unstable when (d2U/dx2)0​=0.

Solution

Solution. At an equilibrium point x0​, U′(x0​)=0. If also U′′(x0​)=0, the second-derivative test is inconclusive and one must look at the first nonvanishing derivative, say of order n, so that near x0​

U(x)≈U(x0​)+n!U(n)(x0​)​(x−x0​)n
  • n even (4, 6, ...): (x−x0​)n≥0 on both sides. If U(n)(x0​)>0, x0​ is a minimum: stable. If U(n)(x0​)<0, it is a maximum: unstable.
  • n odd (3, 5, ...): (x−x0​)n changes sign across x0​, so U increases on one side and decreases on the other: an inflection. The equilibrium is unstable (stable against displacement on one side only).

Equivalently, compute the force F=−U′ on both sides of x0​ (or the sign of U′ just left and right): a restoring force on both sides means stable.

Example: U=x4 has U′′=0 at 0 but U(4)=24>0, so x=0 is stable; U=x3 has U(3)=6=0 at 0, so it is an unstable inflection.

Problem 8 [HW practice · MT 2-47]

A particle moves in the region x>0 under the potential U(x)=U0​(xa​+ax​) with U0​=1 J and a=2 m. Plot the potential, find the equilibrium points, and determine whether they are maxima or minima.

Solution

Solution. With the numbers, U(x)=x2​+2x​ (J, with x in metres).

Equilibrium: F=−dU/dx=0:

dxdU​=U0​(−x2a​+a1​)=0 ⇒ x2=a2 ⇒ x=a=2 m (x>0)

There is only one equilibrium point, with U(a)=2U0​=2 J.

Type: dx2d2U​=x32U0​a​=a22U0​​=0.5 J m−2>0 at x=a: a minimum, so the equilibrium is stable.

Plot:

x / m 0.5 1 2 4 8
U / J 4.25 2.5 2.0 2.5 4.25

U→∞ as x→0+ (the a/x term) and U→∞ linearly as x→∞ (the x/a term): a single well with its bottom at x=2 m. Small oscillations about the minimum have ω2=U′′/m=0.5/m in SI units.

Problem 9 [HW practice · MT 2-53]

Which of the following forces are conservative? If conservative, find the potential energy U(r). (a) Fx​=ayz+bx+c, Fy​=axz+bz, Fz​=axy+by. (b) Fx​=−ze−x, Fy​=lnz, Fz​=e−x+y/z. (c) F=e^r​a/r (a, b, c constants).

Solution

Solution. A force is conservative if ∇×F=0 (on a simply connected region), and then F=−∇U.

(a) ∂y​Fz​−∂z​Fy​=(ax+b)−(ax+b)=0; ∂z​Fx​−∂x​Fz​=ay−ay=0; ∂x​Fy​−∂y​Fx​=az−az=0. Conservative. Integrate Fx​=−∂U/∂x: U=−(axyz+21​bx2+cx)+g(y,z). Then −∂U/∂y=axz−gy​ must equal Fy​=axz+bz, so gy​=−bz; and −∂U/∂z=axy−gz​ must equal Fz​=axy+by, so gz​=−by. Both hold for g=−byz:

U=−(axyz+21​bx2+cx+byz)​

(b) ∂y​Fz​−∂z​Fy​=z1​−z1​=0; ∂z​Fx​−∂x​Fz​=−e−x−(−e−x)=0; ∂x​Fy​−∂y​Fx​=0−0=0. Conservative.

U=−(ze−x+ylnz)​

Check: −∂U/∂x=−ze−x ✓, −∂U/∂y=lnz ✓, −∂U/∂z=e−x+y/z ✓.

(c) A purely radial force depending only on r, F=F(r)e^r​, has zero curl in spherical coordinates, so it is conservative (a central force). With F(r)=a/r:

U(r)=−∫Fdr=−alnr+const​

(The constants b and c in the statement do not appear in this force.)
