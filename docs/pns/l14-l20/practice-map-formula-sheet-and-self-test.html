3. Exam-style self-test

Try each one before looking at the answers that follow.

T1. X has MGF MX​(t)=1−te2(et−1)​ for t<1. Identify the distribution of X, and find E[X] and Var(X).

T2. N is Geometric(41​) on {1,2,…}, and X1​,X2​,… are i.i.d. Exponential(2), independent of N. Find the distribution of S=X1​+⋯+XN​.

T3. Give a sampling algorithm, using U∼U[0,1], for (a) the pdf f(x)=3x2 on [0,1]; (b) the pmf P(X=1)=0.2, P(X=2)=0.5, P(X=3)=0.3.

T4. Use accept–reject with proposal U[0,1] for the target f(x)=6x(1−x) on [0,1]. Find the smallest c, the acceptance probability, and the expected number of proposals needed for 100 accepted samples.

T5. You estimate p=P(A) by the fraction of N independent simulation runs in which A happens. How large must N be so that the standard error is at most 0.005, whatever p is?

T6. Xn​ are independent, with Xn​=n with probability n21​ and Xn​=0 otherwise. Does Xn​→0 (a) almost surely, (b) in probability, (c) in mean (L1), (d) in mean square?

T7. Xn​ is uniform on {n1​,n2​,…,nn​}. Show that Xn​d​U[0,1].

T8. A fair die is rolled 100 times. Approximate the probability that the total is at least 380.

T9. X=(X1​,X2​) has mean (1,−1) and covariance [21​13​]. Let Y1​=X1​+X2​ and Y2​=X1​−2X2​. Find E[Y], CY​ and the correlation of Y1​ and Y2​.

T10. (X,Y) is bivariate normal with μX​=0, μY​=2, σX​=1, σY​=3, ρ=−0.6. Find P(Y>2∣X=1) and P(X+Y>0).

T11. True or false? (a) If Xn​p​X, then E[Xn​]→E[X]. (b) If Xn​d​c for a constant c, then Xn​p​c. (c) A covariance matrix can have a negative entry on its diagonal. (d) If MX​(t)=MY​(t) for all t∈(−1,1), then X and Y have the same distribution. (e) In accept–reject, using a larger c than necessary (still with f≤cg) produces samples with the wrong distribution. (f) If X and Y are each N(0,1) and Cov(X,Y)=0, then X and Y are independent.

Answers

Answers

T1. MX​ is the product of the Poisson(2) MGF e2(et−1) and the Exponential(1) MGF 1−t1​. By uniqueness, X has the distribution of P+E with P∼ Poisson(2) and E∼ Exp(1) independent. With K(t)=2(et−1)−ln(1−t): E[X]=K′(0)=2+1=3 and Var(X)=K′′(0)=2+1=3.

T2. MN​(s)=1−(1−p)espes​ with p=41​, and MX​(t)=2−t2​. Substituting es=MX​(t):

MS​(t)=1−(1−p)2−t2​p⋅2−t2​​=2p−t2p​=1/2−t1/2​.

So S∼ Exponential(21​), with mean 2=E[N]E[X]=4⋅21​. ✓ (A geometric sum of exponentials is exponential.)

T3. (a) F(x)=x3, so X=U1/3. (b) Return 1 if U≤0.2, return 2 if 0.2<U≤0.7, and return 3 otherwise.

T4. c=maxf=f(21​)=1.5. The acceptance probability is c1​=32​, and 100 samples need 100c=150 proposals on average.

T5. The standard error is Np(1−p)​​≤2N​1​, since p(1−p)≤41​. So 2N​1​≤0.005 gives N≥10,000.

T6. (a) Yes: ∑P(Xn​=0)=∑n21​<∞, so by Borel–Cantelli only finitely many Xn​ are non-zero. (b) Yes, since a.s. implies p. (c) Yes: E∣Xn​∣=n⋅n21​=n1​→0. (d) No: E[Xn2​]=n2⋅n21​=1 for every n.

T7. For 0≤x≤1, FXn​​(x)=n⌊nx⌋​, and ​n⌊nx⌋​−x​≤n1​→0. So FXn​​(x)→x, the U[0,1] CDF (and the CDFs are 0 for x<0 and 1 for x>1).

T8. One roll has mean 3.5 and variance 1235​. So S has mean 350 and SD 100⋅1235​​≈17.08. With the continuity correction, P(S≥380)=P(S≥379.5)≈1−Φ(17.0829.5​)=1−Φ(1.73)≈0.042. (The exact value is 0.0420.)

T9. A=[11​1−2​], E[Y]=A(1,−1)T=(0,3)T, and
