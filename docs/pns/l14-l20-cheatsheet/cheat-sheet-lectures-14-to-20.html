Definition and moments

2. Simulation (L14 to L16)

Discrete inverse transform

Values x1​<x2​<… with Fk​=P(X≤xk​): return xk​ when Fk−1​<U≤Fk​ (probability pk​). Average number of comparisons is ∑k​kpk​, so test likely values first.

  • Bernoulli: 1{U≤p}. Binomial: add n Bernoullis.
  • Geometric(p): X=⌈ln(1−p)lnU​⌉.
  • Poisson(λ): recursion pk+1​=pk​k+1λ​ in the search, or multiply uniforms until the product drops below e−λ; X = factors used −1.

Continuous inverse transform

X=F−1(U). In general use G(u)=inf{x:F(x)≥u}, since G(u)≤x⟺u≤F(x), so P(G(U)≤x)=F(x).

Importance sampling

Ef​[h(X)]=Eg​[h(Y)g(Y)f(Y)​]≈N1​i∑​h(Yi​)g(Yi​)f(Yi​)​,Yi​∼g
  • Support condition: g>0 wherever hf=0, otherwise the estimate is silently biased.
  • Best g∝∣h∣f (zero variance when h≥0). The proposal needs tails at least as heavy as hf.
  • Rare events: move g to the region. P(Z>5) with Y∼N(5,1): weight φ(y−5)φ(y)​=e−5y+12.5.

Accept–reject

Need f≤cg (so c≥1 and the support of f lies inside that of g). Draw Y∼g and U; accept X=Y if U≤cg(Y)f(Y)​, else repeat.

  • P(accept)=c1​. Rounds per sample ∼ Geometric(c1​), mean c; k samples take kc rounds on average.
  • Best c=supx​g(x)f(x)​: maximise the ratio by calculus.
  • Unnormalised target f~​ (constant ∫f~​ unknown): bound f~​≤c~g; it still works, with acceptance probability c~∫f~​​.
  • Known values: Beta(2,4) with U: c=64135​. Half-normal with Exp(1): c=2e/π​≈1.32. Gamma(25​,1) with Exp(52​): c≈1.66.

Normal samples

  • Box–Muller: Z1​=−2lnU1​​cos2πU2​ and Z2​=−2lnU1​​sin2πU2​ are i.i.d. N(0,1).
  • Any N(μ,Σ): X=AZ+μ with AAT=Σ (Cholesky). For 2×2: A=[σ1​ρσ2​​0σ2​1−ρ2​​].

3. Convergence (L16 to L18)

Functions: pointwise vs uniform

Pointwise: fn​(x)→f(x) for each x. Uniform: supx​∣fn​(x)−f(x)∣→0; find the sup by calculus.

  • xn on [0,1]: pointwise to 1{x=1}, not uniform (the limit jumps).
  • nxe−nx on [0,∞): pointwise to 0, sup =e1​ at x=n1​: not uniform.
  • 1+nx2x​: sup =2n​1​→0: uniform. nsinnx​: uniform.

Five modes for Xn​→X

Mode Meaning
sure Xn​(ω)→X(ω) for every ω
a.s. P({ω:Xn​(ω)→X(ω)})=1
in probability P(∣Xn​−X∣>ϵ)→0 for all ϵ>0
Lr (r≥1) E∣Xn​−X∣r→0; r=2: mean square
in distribution FXn​​(x)→FX​(x) at continuity points of FX​

Implications: sure ⇒ a.s. ⇒ p ⇒ d, and for s>r≥1, Ls⇒Lr⇒ p. d ⇒ p only when the limit is a constant. Nothing else holds in general.

Counterexamples to memorise

  • p but not a.s.: independent Xn​∼ Bernoulli(n1​): ∑n1​=∞, so infinitely many 1s. Also in m.s. (so m.s. ⇒ a.s.).
  • p but not L1: Xn​=n with probability n1​, else 0: E∣Xn​∣=1.
  • a.s. but not L1: Xn​=n1{U≤n1​} (one U): →0 a.s., E[Xn​]=1.
  • d but not p: X∼N(0,1), Xn​=−X.

How to prove each

  • a.s.: fix ω and find the limit (ωn→0 on [0,1)), or Borel–Cantelli: ∑n​P(∣Xn​−X∣>ϵ)<∞ for every ϵ.
  • p: compute P(∣Xn​−X∣>ϵ), or use Markov / Chebyshev, or show Lr.
  • m.s. to c: E(Xn​−c)2=Var(Xn​)+(E[Xn​]−c)2→0.
  • d: CDFs; or for integer values P(Xn​=k)→P(X=k) for all k; or MGFs.
  • Continuous mapping: g continuous keeps a.s., p and d convergence: g(Xn​)→g(X).

Borel–Cantelli

  • BC1: ∑n​P(An​)<∞⇒P(An​ i.o.)=0 (no independence needed).
  • BC2: An​ independent and ∑n​P(An​)=∞⇒P(An​ i.o.)=1.
  • Independent Bernoulli(pn​): Xn​→0 a.s. exactly when ∑pn​<∞ (n21​ yes, n1​ no).

Inequalities and laws of large numbers

  • Markov: P(X≥a)≤aE[X]​ for X≥0. Chebyshev: P(∣X−μ∣≥c)≤c2σ2​.
  • Sample mean Mn​=nSn​​: E[Mn​]=μ, Var(Mn​)=nσ2​; update Mn​=Mn−1​+n1​(Xn​−Mn−1​).
  • WLLN: P(∣Mn​−μ∣≥ϵ)≤nϵ2σ2​→0. SLLN: Mn​→μ a.s. (fails for Cauchy).
  • Polling (±0.01, 95%): Chebyshev n≥4ϵ2δ1​=50,000; CLT n≥4ϵ21.962​=9604.

Handy limits

  • (1−nλ​)n→e−λ; (1+nx​)n→ex; ∑np1​<∞ iff p>1.
  • n i.i.d. U[0,1]: P(max≤x)=xn, P(min>x)=(1−x)n; n⋅mind​ Exp(1).
  • Bin(n,nλ​)d​ Poisson(λ). U(0,n1​) and N(0,n1​) go to 0 a.s., in p, in m.s. and in d (BC1 gives a.s. whatever the joint construction).

5. Random vectors (L19)

Mean and covariance

  • E[X]=(E[X1​],…,E[Xn​])T and E[AX+b]=AE[X]+b for any m×n matrix A.
  • CX​=E[(X−μ)(X−μ)T]=E[XXT]−μμT, with Cij​=Cov(Xi​,Xj​) and variances on the diagonal.
  • Symmetric and PSD: aTCa=Var(aTX)≥0. Singular exactly when some aTX (a=0) is constant.
CAX+b​=ACX​AT,Cov(aTX,bTX)=aTCb
  • E[(aTX)2]=aTCa+(aTμ)2; correlation ρij​=Cii​Cjj​​Cij​​.
  • Independent components ⇒ diagonal C (the converse needs Gaussian).
  • Total covariance: Cov(Y)=E[Cov(Y∣X)]+Cov(E[Y∣X]). For Y=AX+Z with independent parts: AΣX​AT+ΣZ​.

Jacobian (change of variables)

Y=G(X), G:Rn→Rn invertible, inverse H:

fY​(y)=fX​(H(y))∣J∣,J=det[∂yj​∂Hi​​]
  1. Solve y=G(x) for x=H(y).
  2. J from the partials of H, or J=1/det[∂xj​∂Gi​​] at x=H(y).
  3. Substitute, and take ∣J∣.
  4. State the support: the y whose H(y) lies where fX​>0.
  • Affine: fY​(y)=∣detA∣1​fX​(A−1(y−b)).
  • Sum and difference (X1​+X2​,X1​−X2​): ∣J∣=21​. Polar x=rcosθ, y=rsinθ: fR,Θ​(r,θ)=fX,Y​(x,y)r.
  • Density of one function only (say X1​+X2​): add Y2​=X2​, transform, then integrate y2​ out.
  • det[ac​bd​]=ad−bc and [ac​bd​]−1=ad−bc1​[d−c​−ba​].

6. Gaussian vectors (L20)

Definition, density, MGF

Standard normal vector Z: i.i.d. N(0,1) entries, E[Z]=0, CZ​=I, fZ​(z)=(2π)−n/2e−zTz/2.

X∼N(μ,Σ) means any (all equivalent):

  • X=AZ+μ with AAT=Σ;
  • every aTX∼N(aTμ,aTΣa);
  • (Σ invertible) the pdf
f(x)=(2π)n/2detΣ​1​e−21​(x−μ)TΣ−1(x−μ)

MGF M(s)=esTμ+21​sTΣs: the distribution is fixed by μ and Σ.

Closure properties

  • Affine: AX+b∼N(Aμ+b,AΣAT) for any m×n A. If the result is singular (rank <m) there is no pdf, but every component is still normal.
  • Marginal: keep the rows and columns you want: X1​∼N(μ1​,Σ11​).
  • Conditional:
X1​∣X2​=x2​∼N(μ1​+Σ12​Σ22−1​(x2​−μ2​), Σ11​−Σ12​Σ22−1​Σ21​)

The mean is linear in x2​; the covariance shrinks and does not depend on x2​.

  • Proof trick: W=X1​−Σ12​Σ22−1​X2​ is independent of X2​. Likewise X1​+aX2​ is independent of X2​ iff a=−σ22​σ12​​.
  • Independence: jointly Gaussian and uncorrelated ⇒ independent. Not for merely normal marginals: Y=±X on a fair coin is N(0,1) with Cov(X,Y)=0, yet dependent, and X+Y has an atom at 0.

Bivariate normal

With σ12​=ρσ1​σ2​, u=σ1​x1​−μ1​​, v=σ2​x2​−μ2​​:

f=2πσ1​σ2​1−ρ2​1​exp(−2(1−ρ2)u2−2ρuv+v2​)
X1​∣X2​=x2​∼N(μ1​+ρσ1​σ2​x2​−μ2​​, (1−ρ2)σ12​)
  • Build it: X1​=μ1​+σ1​Z1​ and X2​=μ2​+σ2​(ρZ1​+1−ρ2​Z2​)
  • aX1​+bX2​∼N(aμ1​+bμ2​, a2σ12​+b2σ22​+2abρσ1​σ2​).
  • Equal variances: X1​+X2​ and X1​−X2​ are independent (covariance σ12​−σ22​=0).
  • Contours are ellipses (x−μ)TΣ−1(x−μ)=c; in 2-D the ellipse holds probability 1−e−c/2 (95% at c≈5.99).

7. Exam traps

  1. MX+Y​=MX​MY​ needs independence. An MGF equals 1 at t=0.
  2. Inverse transform inverts the CDF, not the pdf. Keep track of the support.
  3. Importance sampling with g=0 where hf=0 is biased, with no warning.
  4. Accept–reject with a larger c is still exact, just slower (c rounds per sample).
  5. Convergence in probability says nothing about E[Xn​] (n with probability n1​).
  6. In distribution: compare CDFs only at continuity points. Xn​=n1​→0 although Fn​(0)=0=F(0)=1.
  7. BC2 needs independence; BC1 does not.
  8. Continuity correction only for integer-valued sums; ±0.5 in the right direction.
  9. Jacobian: ∣J∣ of the inverse map, written in y, plus the support.
  10. CAX​=ACX​AT (not ATCA); the shift b drops out.
  11. Uncorrelated ⇒ independent only for jointly Gaussian vectors.
