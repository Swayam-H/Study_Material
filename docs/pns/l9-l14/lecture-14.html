Lecture 14

Moment generating functions and sampling a discrete random variable

Deck A, slides 1 to 12, short version · 3 min read

This lecture is covered in full in the companion notes study/L14-L20-notes.md (and .pdf): "Lecture 14, part 1: Moment generating functions" (slides 2 to 6, with 13 extra problems) and the first half of "Lectures 14 and 15: Generating samples from a uniform" (slides 7 to 12). This section is a one-page summary so that this document is complete, and to show how L14 connects to L12 and L13.

What the slides say, in one page

Slides 2 and 3: definition and examples. The moment generating function of X is

MX​(t)=E[etX]=x∑​etxpX​(x)or∫etxfX​(x)dx,

defined for the t where the expectation is finite (the region of convergence, which always contains t=0, where MX​(0)=1). Standard examples:

X MX​(t) where finite
Bernoulli(p) 1−p+pet all t
Binomial(n,p) (1−p+pet)n all t
Geometric(p) 1−(1−p)etpet​ t<−ln(1−p)
Poisson(λ) eλ(et−1) all t
Uniform[a,b] t(b−a)etb−eta​ (=1 at t=0) all t
Exponential(λ) λ−tλ​ t<λ
N(μ,σ2) eμt+σ2t2/2 all t

Slide 4: moments from derivatives. MX(k)​(0)=E[Xk]. So E[X]=M′(0) and Var(X)=M′′(0)−(M′(0))2. Shortcut: with K(t)=lnMX​(t), E[X]=K′(0) and Var(X)=K′′(0).

Key fact (used on slides 5 and 6). If two MGFs agree on an open interval around 0, the distributions are the same. So you can identify a distribution by recognising its MGF.

Slide 5: sums of independent variables. If X and Y are independent,

MX+Y​(t)=E[etXetY]=MX​(t)MY​(t).

This is the L10 fact E[g(X)h(Y)]=E[g(X)]E[h(Y)] with g=h=et⋅. It turns the convolution of L13 into a product. Example: Poisson(μ) + Poisson(λ) has MGF eμ(et−1)eλ(et−1)=e(μ+λ)(et−1), so it is Poisson(μ+λ): compare with the convolution proof in L13 B2.

Slide 6: i.i.d. sums and random sums. For n i.i.d. terms, MX1​+⋯+Xn​​(t)=(MX​(t))n. For a random sum Y=X1​+⋯+XN​ with N independent of the i.i.d. Xi​, condition on N (L12):

MY​(t)=E[E[etY∣N]]=E[(MX​(t))N]=MN​(lnMX​(t)),

i.e. take MN​(s) and replace every es by MX​(t). Differentiating at 0 gives back the L12 slide 29 formulas E[Y]=E[N]E[X] and Var(Y)=E[N]Var(X)+(E[X])2Var(N).

Slides 7 to 12: sampling a discrete random variable from a uniform. Given U∼U[0,1] and a PMF p0​,p1​,… on values x0​<x1​<⋯, output

X=xj​ifi<j∑​pi​≤U<i≤j∑​pi​,

so P(X=xj​)=pj​ (the length of the j-th segment). Since the cut points are the CDF values, this is X=FX−1​(U) with the generalised inverse F−1(u)=inf{x:F(x)≥u}: the inverse transform method. Example: a fair die is X=⌈6U⌉.
