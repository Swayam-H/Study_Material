Mixed problems: several topics at once

Problem 1 [Mixed: Bayes + Poisson + prediction]

The number of emails you get in an hour is Poisson(λ). On a normal day λ=2; on a busy day λ=5. Prior: P(busy)=0.3. In the first hour you get 4 emails. (a) Find P(busy∣X=4). (b) Given this, find the probability that the next hour brings no emails (hours are independent given λ).

Solution

Solution. (a) The 4!1​ cancels, so

P(busy∣X=4)=0.7e−224+0.3e−5540.3e−554​=0.7(0.13534)(16)+1.26340.3(0.006738)(625)​=1.5157+1.26341.2634​≈0.455​.

Four emails is evidence for a busy day: the probability rose from 0.3 to 0.455.

(b) Condition on the type of day, using the posterior weights (total probability):

P(X2​=0∣X1​=4)=0.545e−2+0.455e−5=0.0738+0.0031≈0.077​.

Note X1​ and X2​ are independent given λ, but not independent: X1​ tells you about λ, which tells you about X2​.

Problem 2 [Mixed: joint pdf + covariance + CLT]

(X,Y) has joint pdf f(x,y)=x+y on [0,1]2. (a) Find E[X], Var(X) and Cov(X,Y). (b) Find E[X+Y] and Var(X+Y). (c) S is the sum of 100 independent copies of X+Y. Approximate P(S>120).

Solution

Solution. (a) Marginal: fX​(x)=∫01​(x+y)dy=x+21​. Then

E[X]=∫01​x(x+21​)dx=31​+41​=127​,E[X2]=41​+61​=125​,Var(X)=125​−14449​=14411​.

E[XY]=∫∫xy(x+y)=2∫01​∫01​x2ydxdy=2⋅31​⋅21​=31​, so

Cov(X,Y)=31​−(127​)2=14448−49​=−1441​.

By symmetry Y has the same mean and variance.

(b) E[X+Y]=67​ and Var(X+Y)=14411​+14411​+2(−1441​)=14420​=365​. Forgetting the covariance term is the classic slip.

(c) E[S]=6700​≈116.67, Var(S)=36500​≈13.89, sd ≈3.727. By the CLT,

P(S>120)≈P(Z>3.727120−116.67​)=P(Z>0.89)≈0.19​.

Problem 3 [Mixed: uniform prior + iterated expectation + Beta posterior] (hard)

A coin's bias P is Uniform(0,1). Given P=p, you toss it n times and get K heads. (a) Show P(K=k)=n+11​ for k=0,…,n. (b) Find E[K] and Var(K) by conditioning on P. (c) Find the posterior of P given K=k and the CE estimate.

Solution

Solution. (a) By total probability over the continuous P,

P(K=k)=∫01​(kn​)pk(1−p)n−kdp=(kn​)B(k+1,n−k+1)=(kn​)(n+1)!k!(n−k)!​=n+11​.

(b) E[K∣P]=nP and Var(K∣P)=nP(1−P). So

E[K]=E[nP]=2n​,Var(K)=E[nP(1−P)]+Var(nP)=n(21​−31​)+12n2​=6n​+12n2​​.

Check with (a): K uniform on {0,…,n} has variance 12(n+1)2−1​=12n2+2n​, the same.

(c) Posterior ∝pk(1−p)n−k⋅1, which is Beta(k+1,n−k+1). CE =n+2k+1​ (Laplace's rule of succession).

Problem 4 [Mixed: MLE + CLT + an approximate interval]

Waiting times are i.i.d. Exp(λ). From n=100 waits you find xˉ=2 minutes. (a) Find the MLE of λ and of the mean wait μ=1/λ. (b) Use the CLT to give an approximate 95% range for μ, and turn it into one for λ.

Solution

Solution. (a) λ^=1/xˉ=0.5 per minute, and by invariance μ^​=xˉ=2 minutes.

(b) Xˉ≈N(μ,nμ2​), since an exponential's sd equals its mean. Plugging in μ^​=2 for the sd, sd(Xˉ)≈102​=0.2, so with probability about 0.95, ∣Xˉ−μ∣≤1.96(0.2)=0.392:

μ∈[1.61, 2.39]⟹λ=μ1​∈[0.42, 0.62].

(Taking reciprocals flips the ends.) The range shrinks like 1/n​: four times the data halves it.

Problem 5 [Mixed: polar Jacobian + Rayleigh + MLE]

X,Y are i.i.d. N(0,σ2) and R=X2+Y2​. (a) Find the pdf of R. (b) You observe R1​,…,Rn​ i.i.d. like R. Find the MLE of σ2 and check whether it is unbiased.

Solution

Solution. (a) Change to polar coordinates, x=rcosϕ, y=rsinϕ, with Jacobian r:

fR,Φ​(r,ϕ)=2πσ21​e−r2/(2σ2)⋅r,r>0, 0≤ϕ<2π.

It factors, so R and Φ are independent, and integrating out ϕ gives

fR​(r)=σ2r​e−r2/(2σ2),r>0(Rayleigh).

(b) lnL=∑lnri​−nlnσ2−2σ2∑ri2​​. Differentiate in v=σ2: −vn​+2v2∑ri2​​=0, so

σ^2=2n1​i=1∑n​Ri2​.

E[R2]=E[X2+Y2]=2σ2, so E[σ^2]=σ2: unbiased.

Problem 6 [Mixed: CLT inside Bayes' rule]

Machine A makes bolts with mean length 10.0 mm, machine B with mean 10.3 mm; both have sd 0.5 mm. A box comes from one machine, each equally likely. You measure 25 bolts and get xˉ=10.2. Using the CLT for Xˉ, find P(A∣xˉ).

Solution

Solution. Given the machine, Xˉ≈N(μ,250.25​), sd 0.1. Treat the normal density as the likelihood:

A: z=0.110.2−10.0​=2,B: z=0.110.2−10.3​=−1.

The common factors cancel, and with equal priors

P(A∣xˉ)=e−22/2+e−12/2e−22/2​=0.1353+0.60650.1353​≈0.18​.

So B is about 4.5 times as likely. The single bolts overlap a lot, but averaging 25 of them makes the evidence sharp.

Problem 7 [Mixed: random sums + total variance + Bayes]

Roll a fair die to get X, then toss X fair coins and let Y be the number of heads. (a) Find E[Y] and Var(Y). (b) Find Cov(X,Y). (c) Find P(X=6∣Y=5).

Solution

Solution. Given X, Y∼Binomial(X,21​), so E[Y∣X]=2X​ and Var(Y∣X)=4X​.

(a) E[Y]=23.5​=1.75, and by the law of total variance

Var(Y)=E[4X​]+Var(2X​)=43.5​+41​⋅1235​=87​+4835​=4877​​≈1.60.

(b) E[XY]=E[XE[Y∣X]]=21​E[X2], so Cov(X,Y)=21​E[X2]−E[X]⋅2E[X]​=21​Var(X)=2435​.

(c) Only X=5 or 6 can give 5 heads. P(Y=5∣X=5)=321​ and P(Y=5∣X=6)=646​. The prior 61​ cancels:

P(X=6∣Y=5)=2/64+6/646/64​=43​​.

Problem 8 [Mixed: MGFs + the Gamma sum + exact vs CLT]

X1​,X2​,… are i.i.d. Exp(1) and Sn​=X1​+⋯+Xn​. (a) Use MGFs to identify the distribution of Sn​. (b) Compute P(S2​>3) exactly and by the CLT. (c) Approximate P(S100​>110).

Solution

Solution. (a) MX​(t)=1−t1​ for t<1. By independence,

MSn​​(t)=(MX​(t))n=(1−t)−n,t<1,

which is the MGF of Gamma(n,1). By uniqueness of MGFs, Sn​∼ Gamma(n,1), with pdf (n−1)!sn−1e−s​ for s>0.

(b) Exact: S2​ has pdf se−s. Integrating by parts,

P(S2​>3)=∫3∞​se−sds=[−se−s−e−s]3∞​=4e−3≈0.199.

CLT: mean 2, variance 2, so P(S2​>3)≈P(Z>2​1​)=P(Z>0.71)≈0.24. That is 20% too high, because S2​ is still very skewed.

(c) Mean 100, sd 10:

P(S100​>110)≈P(Z>1)≈0.159.

The exact Gamma answer is about 0.158, so here the CLT is within 0.001. The CLT is a large-n statement.

Problem 9 [Mixed: MLE + convergence in probability and in distribution] (hard)

X1​,…,Xn​ i.i.d. Uniform[0,θ] and Mn​=maxi​Xi​ (the MLE). (a) Show Mn​→θ in probability. (b) Show n(θ−Mn​) converges in distribution, and identify the limit.

Solution

Solution. Everything follows from P(Mn​≤m)=(θm​)n for 0≤m≤θ (all n samples must be ≤m).

(a) Mn​≤θ always, so for 0<ϵ<θ,

P(∣Mn​−θ∣>ϵ)=P(Mn​<θ−ϵ)=(1−θϵ​)n→0.

So Mn​p​θ: the MLE is consistent.

(b) Let Wn​=n(θ−Mn​)≥0. For x≥0 and n large enough that x<nθ,

P(Wn​>x)=P(Mn​<θ−nx​)=(1−nθx​)n→e−x/θ.

So

n(θ−Mn​)d​Exp(θ1​)(rate θ1​, mean θ).

The error of Mn​ is of size n1​, not the usual n​1​, which is why its MSE (Part 7, Problem 21) shrinks like n21​.

Problem 10 [Mixed: Gaussian vectors + conditional expectation + MAP]

A signal Θ∼N(0,1) is sent and you receive X=Θ+W, with noise W∼N(0,σ2) independent of Θ. (a) Find the joint distribution of (Θ,X). (b) Find E[Θ∣X] and the MAP estimate. (c) Compare its MSE with that of the ML estimate θ^=X.

Solution

Solution. (a) (Θ,X) is a linear map of the independent Gaussians (Θ,W), so it is jointly Gaussian, with means 0, Var(Θ)=1, Var(X)=1+σ2 and Cov(Θ,X)=Var(Θ)=1.

(b) For a bivariate normal, E[Θ∣X=x]=Var(X)Cov(Θ,X)​x=1+σ2x​. The posterior is normal, so its mode equals its mean: MAP = CE =1+σ2x​.

(c) The conditional variance is 1−1+σ21​=1+σ2σ2​, which is the MSE of the CE. The ML estimate X has MSE E[W2]=σ2, which is larger. With σ2=1: 0.5 against 1. Shrinking towards the prior mean pays off.

Problem 11 [Mixed: counting + hypergeometric likelihood + MLE]

To estimate the number N of fish in a lake, you tag 10 and release them. Later you catch 20 and find 4 tagged. Find the MLE of N.

Solution

Solution. Given N, the number tagged in the catch is hypergeometric:

L(N)=(20N​)(410​)(16N−10​)​,N≥26.

N is an integer, so compare neighbours instead of differentiating:

L(N−1)L(N)​=N−26N−10​⋅NN−20​ ≥1⟺(N−10)(N−20)≥N(N−26)⟺200≥4N⟺N≤50.

So L rises up to N=50 and then falls, with L(50)=L(49). Both 49 and 50 are MLEs. This matches the intuition 204​=N10​, giving N≈50.

Problem 12 [Mixed: minimum of exponentials + MLE + bias]

A device has two parts in series, each with an Exp(λ) lifetime, independently. It fails when either part fails. You observe device lifetimes T1​,…,Tn​. (a) Find the distribution of T. (b) Find the MLE of λ. (c) Is it unbiased? If not, fix it.

Solution

Solution. (a) P(T>t)=P(both parts survive past t)=e−2λt, so T∼Exp(2λ).

(b) lnL=nln(2λ)−2λ∑ti​, so λn​=2∑ti​ and λ^=2∑Ti​n​=2Tˉ1​.

(c) ∑Ti​∼Gamma(n,2λ), and E[1/Gamma(n,r)]=n−1r​. So E[λ^]=2n​⋅n−12λ​=n−1n​λ: biased upwards. The fix is 2∑Ti​n−1​.

Problem 13 [Mixed: mixtures + Bayes + total variance]

X comes from N(0,1) or N(2,1), each with probability 21​. (a) Find E[X] and Var(X). (b) You observe x=1.5. Which component most likely produced it?

Solution

Solution. (a) Let C be the component. E[X]=21​(0)+21​(2)=1. By total variance,

Var(X)=E[Var(X∣C)]+Var(E[X∣C])=1+Var(0 or 2, fifty-fifty)=1+1=2​.

(b) With equal priors the 2π​1​ cancels:

P(C=2∣x=1.5)=e−(1.5)2/2+e−(0.5)2/2e−(0.5)2/2​=1+e−11​≈0.73​.

The second component is about 2.7 times as likely. (e−1.125/e−0.125=e−1.)

Problem 14 [Mixed: bivariate normal + best linear predictor + least squares]

(X,Y) is bivariate normal with μX​=0, μY​=1, σX​=1, σY​=2 and ρ=0.5. (a) Find E[Y∣X=x]. (b) Find the a,b that minimise E[(Y−a−bX)2]. (c) How does this connect to least squares on data?

Solution

Solution. (a) For a bivariate normal,

E[Y∣X=x]=μY​+ρσX​σY​​(x−μX​)=1+x.

(b) Set the derivatives to zero, exactly as on regression slides 12–20 but with expectations instead of sums:

a=E[Y]−bE[X],b=Var(X)Cov(X,Y)​=10.5⋅1⋅2​=1,

so a=1. The best line is 1+x, the same as (a).

(c) Least squares replaces E by averages: β^​1​=Sxx​Sxy​​ is the sample version of Var(X)Cov(X,Y)​, and β^​0​=yˉ​−β^​1​xˉ is the sample version of a. For Gaussian data, the fitted line estimates E[Y∣X].

Problem 15 [Mixed: Monte Carlo + CLT + sample size]

You estimate p=P(Z>2) for Z∼N(0,1) by simulating n normals and taking the fraction above 2. (a) What are the mean and standard error of the estimate? (b) How large must n be for the estimate to be within 0.001 of p≈0.0228 with probability about 0.95?

Solution

Solution. (a) The estimate is p^​=n1​∑i=1n​1{Zi​>2}, an average of i.i.d. Bernoulli(p) variables. So

E[p^​]=p  (unbiased),se(p^​)=np(1−p)​​.

(b) By the CLT, p^​≈N(p,np(1−p)​), so P(∣p^​−p∣≤0.001)≈0.95 when 1.96np(1−p)​​≤0.001:

n≥0.00121.962p(1−p)​=10−63.8416×0.0228×0.9772​≈85,600​.

Rare events need many samples, because the error must be small compared with p itself.
