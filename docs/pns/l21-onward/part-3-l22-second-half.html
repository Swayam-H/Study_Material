Part 3 — L22 (second half)

Maximum likelihood

8 min read

Slide 21: the likelihood function

So far we've been checking estimators someone handed us. But where do good estimators come from? For the mean and variance, we had natural guesses. For a general parameter (a rate, a shape, the θ in some weird pdf), we need a systematic recipe. That recipe is maximum likelihood.

The likelihood is the joint pmf/pdf of the data, but viewed as a function of the parameter θ, with the data held fixed:

L(x1​,…,xn​;θ)={pX1​,…,Xn​​(x1​,…,xn​;θ)fX1​,…,Xn​​(x1​,…,xn​;θ)​discretecontinuous​

For i.i.d. data it factorises:

L(θ)=i=1∏n​fX​(xi​;θ),lnL(θ)=i=1∑n​lnfX​(xi​;θ).

The mental shift. The pdf f(x;θ) asks "for this fixed θ, how likely is each x?" The likelihood L(θ) asks "for this fixed data, how well does each θ explain it?" Same formula, different variable. Note that L(θ) is not a pdf in θ; it doesn't have to integrate to 1.

Why the log? Products are painful to differentiate, sums are easy. And since ln is strictly increasing, whatever θ maximises L also maximises lnL. (That's last year's HW 11 Q4, word for word.)

Slide 21 exercise: write down the likelihoods

(a) Exp(θ), rate θ:

L(θ)=∏θe−θxi​=θne−θ∑xi​,xi​≥0.

(b) N(θ,1):

L(θ)=(2π)−n/2exp(−21​∑(xi​−θ)2).

(c) Binom(n0​,θ), n0​ known (I'm calling the trials n0​ so it doesn't clash with the sample size n):

L(θ)=i=1∏n​(xi​n0​​)θxi​(1−θ)n0​−xi​=[∏(xi​n0​​)]θ∑xi​(1−θ)nn0​−∑xi​.

(d) Binom(θ,p), unknown number of trials θ∈{0,1,2,…}:

L(θ)=i=1∏n​(xi​θ​)pxi​(1−p)θ−xi​,and L(θ)=0 if θ<imax​xi​.

Case (d) is the one that's actually interesting, and it's what slide 22's warning is about.

Slide 22: the MLE

Θ^ML​=argθmax​L(x1​,…,xn​;θ)=argθmax​lnL(x1​,…,xn​;θ)​

"Choose the parameter value under which the data you actually saw is most probable."

The default method: set dθd​lnL=0, solve, and check it's a max (second derivative <0, or argue from the shape).

When calculus doesn't work (the slide's warning, plus one more):

  1. The parameter is an integer (like θ in Binom(θ,p)). You can't differentiate in θ. Use the ratio trick: look at L(θ)/L(θ−1) and find where it crosses 1.
  2. The support depends on θ (like Uniform(0,θ)). The likelihood has a jump, and the max sits at a boundary, not at a zero of the derivative. See slide 27.

Worked example: the integer case, Binom(N,p) with N unknown

Take one observation x from Binom(N,p) with p known. Then, for N≥x,

L(N−1)L(N)​=(xN−1​)px(1−p)N−1−x(xN​)px(1−p)N−x​=N−xN​(1−p).

(using (xN​)/(xN−1​)=N−xN​). This ratio is ≥1 exactly when

N(1−p)≥N−x⟺N≤px​.

So L increases while N≤x/p and decreases after: N^ML​=⌊x/p⌋. (If x/p is an integer, both x/p and x/p−1 tie.) Sanity check: you expect x≈Np, so N≈x/p.

Slide 23: properties of the MLE (no proof)

Under mild regularity conditions:

  1. Consistent: Θ^ML​p​θ∗.
  2. Asymptotically unbiased: E[Θ^ML​]→θ∗.

Note what's not on the list: the MLE is not necessarily unbiased for finite n. Examples coming up: σ^ML2​ (divides by n), and θ^ML​=maxxi​ for the uniform.

Two more facts worth knowing (not on the slides, but they come up in problems):

  • Invariance: the MLE of g(θ) is g(θ^ML​). E.g. if θ^ML​=xˉ is the MLE of an exponential's mean, the MLE of the rate 1/θ is 1/xˉ. (Tutorial 11 Q3.)
  • The MLE may not exist, or may not be unique. Examples in the practice section (Tutorial 11 Q8 and HW 11 Q10).

Slides 25–30: the standard MLE examples

These are all on the slides. Do each one yourself before reading; they're quick and very likely exam material.

Exponential (slide 25)

lnL(λ)=nlnλ−λ∑xi​,dλd​lnL=λn​−∑xi​=0⟹λ^ML​=∑xi​n​=xˉ1​​

Second derivative: −n/λ2<0, so it's a max. Makes sense: the mean of Exp(λ) is 1/λ, so estimate the rate as 1 over the sample mean.

Twist: is λ^ML​ unbiased? No. E[1/Xˉ]=1/E[Xˉ] (a nonlinear function again). To get the actual value: T=∑Xi​∼Gamma(n,λ) (L14), so using the Gamma integral ∫0∞​ta−1e−λtdt=λaΓ(a)​,

E[T1​]=∫0∞​t1​⋅Γ(n)λn​tn−1e−λtdt=Γ(n)λn​⋅λn−1Γ(n−1)​=n−1λ​(n≥2).

So E[λ^ML​]=E[n/T]=n−1n​λ. Biased high, but the bias goes to 0, exactly as slide 23 promised.

Bernoulli (slide 26)

lnL(p)=(∑xi​)lnp+(n−∑xi​)ln(1−p),
dpd​lnL=p∑xi​​−1−pn−∑xi​​=0⟹p^​ML​=n1​∑xi​​

(To solve: cross-multiply, (1−p)∑xi​=p(n−∑xi​), and the p∑xi​ terms cancel, leaving ∑xi​=np.)

The fraction of 1s. Unbiased (E[Xˉ]=p) and consistent (SLLN).

Uniform(0,θ) (slide 27) — the "don't differentiate" one

L(θ)=∏θ1​1{0≤xi​≤θ}={θ−n0​θ≥maxxi​θ<maxxi​​

If you blindly differentiate θ−n, you get −nθ−n−1=0 and you're stuck. Instead, look at the function: it's zero until θ=maxxi​ (any smaller θ would make some data point impossible), then it's θ−n, which is decreasing. So the max is at the left edge:

θ^ML​=max(x1​,…,xn​)​

We showed in slide 19 that this is biased (E=n+1n​θ) but consistent. Last year's Endsem Q5 generalised this to Uniform[a,b] with both unknown (practice section).

Normal, σ2 known (slide 28)

lnL(μ)=−2n​ln(2πσ2)−2σ21​∑(xi​−μ)2,dμd​lnL=σ21​∑(xi​−μ)=0⟹μ^​ML​=xˉ​

Notice that maximising lnL here is the same as minimising ∑(xi​−μ)2. That's "least squares", and it comes back in linear regression.

Normal, both unknown (slide 29)

Now lnL(μ,σ2)=−2n​ln(2πσ2)−2σ21​∑(xi​−μ)2, and you set both partial derivatives to zero.

  • ∂/∂μ: same as before, gives μ^​=xˉ (it doesn't depend on σ2).
  • ∂/∂σ2: treat v=σ2 as the variable.
    ∂v∂​lnL=−2vn​+2v21​∑(xi​−μ^​)2=0⟹σ^ML2​=n1​∑(xi​−xˉ)2​

So the MLE of the variance divides by n: it's exactly S2 from slide 16, which we know is biased (E=nn−1​σ2). The MLE gave us the biased one! That's fine; slide 23 only promised asymptotic unbiasedness.

Poisson (slide 30)

Each term of the likelihood is xi​!e−λλxi​​, so

lnL(λ)=∑(xi​lnλ−λ−lnxi​!)=nxˉlnλ−nλ+C,

where C=−∑lnxi​! doesn't involve λ and can be ignored. Differentiate:

λnxˉ​−n=0⟹λ^ML​=xˉ​

Unbiased, with MSE=Var(Xˉ)=λ/n. (That's last year's Endsem Q6, practically verbatim.)

The pattern

For lots of standard families, the MLE ends up matching the sample version of the parameter's meaning: Bernoulli p = mean, so p^​=xˉ. Poisson λ = mean, so λ^=xˉ. Exponential rate = 1/mean, so λ^=1/xˉ. That's a great sanity check. But it's not a rule: the uniform's MLE is the max, not 2xˉ.
