Self-test

Exam-style, roughly in increasing difficulty. Answers are below. Give each one a real attempt first.

  1. True or false, with a one-line reason each: (a) every unbiased estimator is consistent; (b) every consistent estimator is asymptotically unbiased; (c) the MLE is always unbiased; (d) with a uniform prior, MAP = MLE.
  2. X≥0 with E[X]=3. Give the best bound Markov provides on P(X≥12). If you also know Var(X)=1, what does Chebyshev give?
  3. X1​,…,Xn​ i.i.d. with pdf f(x;θ)=e−(x−θ) for x≥θ (a shifted exponential). Find the MLE of θ, its bias, and its MSE. Is it consistent?
  4. X1​,…,Xn​ i.i.d. with pdf f(x;θ)=θx​e−x2/(2θ), x>0 (Rayleigh). Find θ^ML​. Is it unbiased? (Hint: E[X2]=2θ.)
  5. A coin with a Beta(2,2) prior on P(H) shows 7 heads in 10 tosses. Find the posterior, then θ^ML​, θ^MAP​ and θ^CE​, and order them.
  6. Show that for the Beta–Binomial model, the posterior mean is a weighted average of the prior mean and the MLE, and find the weights.
  7. X1​,…,Xn​ are i.i.d. Poisson(λ), with a Gamma(α,β) prior on λ. The CE estimate turns out to equal the MLE exactly. What must be true of the data?

Answers

Answers

1. (a) False: X1​ is unbiased, not consistent. (b) False: the "θ∗ w.p. 1−n1​, n w.p. n1​" example is consistent but E→θ∗+1. (c) False: σ^ML2​ divides by n; maxxi​ for the uniform. (d) True, as long as the MLE lies in the prior's support (the prior term is a constant there).

2. Markov: P(X≥12)≤123​=41​. Chebyshev: P(X≥12)≤P(∣X−3∣≥9)≤811​. Knowing the variance helps a lot.

3. L(θ)=e−∑xi​+nθ if θ≤minxi​, else 0. It's increasing in θ, so θ^=minxi​ (another boundary MLE). minXi​−θ∼Exp(n) (the min of n Exp(1)s), so the bias is n1​ and MSE=n21​+n21​=n22​. MSE →0, so consistent.

4. lnL=∑lnxi​−nlnθ−2θ∑xi2​​; −θn​+2θ2∑xi2​​=0⇒θ^=2n∑xi2​​. E[θ^]=2nn⋅2θ​=θ: unbiased. (A rare MLE that's exactly unbiased.)

5. Posterior Beta(9,5). MLE =0.7, MAP =128​=0.667, CE =149​=0.643. Order: CE < MAP < MLE. The prior (centred at 21​) pulls everything towards 21​, and the mean gets pulled more than the mode.

6. α+β+nα+k​=α+β+nα+β​⋅α+βα​+α+β+nn​⋅nk​. The prior acts like α+β pseudo-observations.

7. β+nα+∑xi​​=xˉ⟺α+nxˉ=βxˉ+nxˉ⟺xˉ=βα​. The sample mean must equal the prior mean. (That's the weighted-average formula: if both things being averaged are equal, so is the average.)
