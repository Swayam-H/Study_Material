Part 6 — Linear regression (skim)

⏱ 3 min read

You said this one isn't a big deal for the exam, so here's just the storyline and the key formulas. The neat thing is that it's a reunion: every idea from L21–L24 shows up again.

The model. Data pairs (xi​,yi​) (e.g. hours studied, marks). We assume

Yi​=β0∗​+β1∗​xi​+εi​,εi​ i.i.d., E[εi​]=0, Var(εi​)=σ2.

The unknowns (β0∗​,β1∗​) are our θ∗.

Least squares. Pick the line minimising the sum of squared residuals J(β0​,β1​)=∑(yi​−β0​−β1​xi​)2. Setting both partials to zero gives

β^​1​=Sxx​Sxy​​,β^​0​=yˉ​−β^​1​xˉ​,Sxx​=∑(xi​−xˉ)2,  Sxy​=∑(xi​−xˉ)(yi​−yˉ​).

(The first equation, ∂J/∂β0​=0, says the line passes through (xˉ,yˉ​).)

Least squares = MLE under Gaussian noise. If εi​∼N(0,σ2), then lnL=−2n​ln(2πσ2)−2σ21​J(β0​,β1​), so maximising lnL is minimising J. Same thing we saw for the normal mean on slide 28.

MLE of the noise variance: σ^ML2​=n1​∑ei2​, where ei​=yi​−β^​0​−β^​1​xi​. Biased, just like the divide-by-n sample variance. The unbiased version divides by n−2 (two parameters estimated, two "degrees of freedom" lost, the same reason as n−1 before).

Different noise ⇒ different loss. With Laplace noise f(e)=2b1​e−∣e∣/b, the MLE minimises ∑∣ei​∣ instead (least absolute deviations). Squares punish outliers hard; absolute values don't. It's the mean-vs-median story.

Bias and MSE (treating the xi​ as fixed, only the noise random): both estimators are unbiased, and

MSE(β^​1​)=Sxx​σ2​,MSE(β^​0​)=σ2(n1​+Sxx​xˉ2​).

So spreading your xi​ out (big Sxx​) pins down the slope better. Makes sense.

Bayesian view = ridge regression. Put independent N(0,τ2) priors on β0​,β1​. Then

ln(posterior)=const−2σ21​J(β0​,β1​)−2τ2β02​+β12​​,

so the MAP minimises J+λ(β02​+β12​) with λ=σ2/τ2. With centred inputs (xˉ=0):

β^​0,MAP​=n+λ∑yi​​,β^​1,MAP​=Sxx​+λSxy​​.

The prior adds λ to each denominator, shrinking the estimates towards 0. With λ=1, β^​0,MAP​=n+1∑yi​​, which is exactly Example 2 with μ0​=0. And because the posterior is Gaussian, CE = MAP again.

That's all you need from this deck. If anything comes up, it'll most likely be "show least squares is the MLE under Gaussian noise", or "show the MAP is the penalised least squares".
