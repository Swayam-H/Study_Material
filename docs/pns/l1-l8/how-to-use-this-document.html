How to use this document

⏱ 6 min read

What it covers. This year's Lectures 1 to 8 (Prof. Tejas Bodas): sample spaces and events, set theory, the axioms of probability, σ-algebras and Borel sets, continuity of P, conditional probability, Bayes' rule, independence, counting, random variables as measurable functions, discrete random variables (PMF, CDF, expectation, variance, the standard families), and continuous random variables (pdf, uniform, exponential, Gaussian, functions of a random variable).

How each lecture is laid out.

  1. Part A, the slides. Every slide in order, with the slide number (for example Slide 41/73), what it says, and an explanation. Every question asked on a slide is answered, every example is worked out, and every red HW is solved.
  2. Part B, extra worked examples and problems. These come from probabilitycourse.com (Pishro-Nik) and from Bertsekas & Tsitsiklis, Introduction to Probability, 2nd ed. (the PDF in probability-and-statistics/textbooks/). Every problem has a full solution. Many are last year's homework, tutorial or exam questions, and those are marked.

Tip. Cover each solution and try the first two lines yourself. In this part of the course most marks are lost on setting up the problem (what is Ω, which events, which formula), not on the algebra.

Five habits that save marks

  1. Write down Ω and the events in symbols before computing anything. Most wrong answers in L3 come from an unclear sample space.
  2. "At least one" means complement. P(at least one)=1−P(none).
  3. Check every PMF sums to 1 and every pdf integrates to 1. It catches most algebra slips.
  4. For a function of a continuous random variable, go through the CDF. FY​(y)=P(g(X)≤y), rewrite as an event about X, then differentiate. It works even when g is not monotone.
  5. State the support. "fY​(y)=… for y∈[e2a,e2b], and 0 otherwise." Last year's Quiz 1 solutions write the support out every time.
