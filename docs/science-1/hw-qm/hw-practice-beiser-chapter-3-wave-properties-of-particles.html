HW practice: Beiser Chapter 3, Wave properties of particles

9 min read · 16 practice problems

Solutions stay collapsed until you click them.

In-chapter examples 3.1 to 3.9 and end-of-chapter problems 1, 7, 14, 20, 23, 29, 33. Constants: ℏ=1.055×10−34 J s, me​=9.11×10−31 kg, mp​=1.673×10−27 kg, mp​c2=938.3 MeV. Uncertainty relation used: ΔxΔp≥ℏ/2.

Problem 1 [HW practice · Beiser Ex 3.1]

Find the de Broglie wavelengths of (a) a 46 g golf ball at 30 m/s, and (b) an electron at 107 m/s.

Solution

Solution. λ=h/mv.

(a) λ=(0.046)(30)6.63×10−34​=4.8×10−34 m​, absurdly small compared with the ball or any aperture, so no wave behaviour is observable.

(b) λ=(9.11×10−31)(107)6.63×10−34​=7.3×10−11 m​, comparable to atomic dimensions, so diffraction is observable (v≪c, so the non-relativistic formula is fine).

Problem 2 [HW practice · Beiser Ex 3.2]

Find the kinetic energy of a proton whose de Broglie wavelength is 1.000 fm.

Solution

Solution. pc=hc/λ=1240 MeV fm/1.000 fm=1240 MeV, far greater than mp​c2=938 MeV, so use relativistic energy:

KE=(pc)2+(mp​c2)2​−mp​c2=12402+938.32​−938.3=1555−938=617 MeV​

A non-relativistic p2/2m would give 820 MeV, which is clearly wrong.

Problem 3 [HW practice · Beiser Ex 3.3]

An electron has de Broglie wavelength 2.00 pm. Find its kinetic energy and the phase and group velocities of its de Broglie waves.

Solution

Solution. pc=hc/λ=1.240 MeV pm/2.00 pm=0.620 MeV, comparable to me​c2=0.511 MeV, so relativistic:

E=0.6202+0.5112​=0.803 MeV,KE=E−mc2=0.292 MeV​

Particle speed: v/c=pc/E=0.620/0.803=0.772, so v=2.31×108 m/s.

The group velocity equals the particle speed, vg​=v=2.31×108 m/s​. The phase velocity is

vp​=pE​=vc2​=3.89×108 m/s​

which exceeds c; this is allowed because no energy or information travels at vp​.

Problem 4 [HW practice · Beiser Ex 3.4]

An electron is in a box 0.10 nm across. Find its permitted energies.

Solution

Solution. For a particle confined to 0<x<L with infinite walls, λn​=2L/n and

En​=8mL2n2h2​=8(9.11×10−31)(10−10)2n2(6.63×10−34)2​=6.0×10−18n2 J=37.6n2 eV​

so E1​=38 eV, E2​=150 eV, E3​=338 eV. The level spacing is of order tens of eV, so atoms have well-separated levels.

Problem 5 [HW practice · Beiser Ex 3.5]

A 10 g marble is in a box 10 cm across. Find its permitted energies.

Solution

Solution. With m=1.0×10−2 kg and L=0.10 m,

En​=8mL2n2h2​=5.5×10−64n2 J​

The minimum energy (n=1) corresponds to a speed of 2E/m​≈10−31 m/s, far too slow to detect, and the levels are so close together that energy appears continuous. Quantization is unobservable for macroscopic objects.

Problem 6 [HW practice · Beiser Ex 3.6]

The position of a proton is established with accuracy 1.00×10−11 m. Find the uncertainty in its position 1.00 s later. Assume v≪c.

Solution

Solution. Initially Δx0​=1.00×10−11 m, so

Δp≥2Δx0​ℏ​=5.27×10−24 kg m s−1,Δv=mp​Δp​=3.15×103 m/s

After t=1.00 s the position spread is

Δx=Δx0​+Δvt≈3.2×103 m​

Pinning a proton down to atomic size makes its velocity so uncertain that after one second it could be several kilometres away.

Problem 7 [HW practice · Beiser Ex 3.7]

A typical atomic nucleus has radius 5.0×10−15 m. Use the uncertainty principle to put a lower limit on the energy an electron must have to be in a nucleus.

Solution

Solution. Take Δx=5.0×10−15 m:

Δp≥2Δxℏ​=1.05×10−20 kg m/s

so pc≥(1.05×10−20)(3.00×108)/(1.60×10−19)=19.7 MeV, much greater than me​c2=0.511 MeV. The electron is ultra-relativistic, E≈pc:

E≳20 MeV​

Electrons emitted in beta decay have energies of only a few MeV, so electrons cannot be confined inside nuclei. Protons and neutrons, being 2000 times heavier, need far less kinetic energy to be confined.

Problem 8 [HW practice · Beiser Ex 3.8]

A hydrogen atom has radius 5.3×10−11 m. Use the uncertainty principle to estimate the minimum energy of the electron.

Solution

Solution. Take Δx=r=5.3×10−11 m, so Δp≥ℏ/2Δx=9.95×10−25 kg m/s. The momentum is at least about this large:

KEmin​≈2m(Δp)2​=2(9.11×10−31)(9.95×10−25)2​=5.4×10−19 J=3.4 eV​

The electron's potential energy is −e2/4πϵ0​r=−27 eV, so total E≈−24 eV: the right order of magnitude (actual −13.6 eV). The atom does not collapse because squeezing Δx raises KE∝1/Δx2 faster than the Coulomb term lowers PE∝−1/Δx.

Problem 9 [HW practice · Beiser Ex 3.9]

An excited atom radiates after an average of 1.0×10−8 s. Find the inherent uncertainty in the frequency of the photon.

Solution

Solution. ΔEΔt≥ℏ/2 with Δt=1.0×10−8 s:

ΔE≥2Δtℏ​=5.3×10−27 J,Δν=hΔE​=4πΔt1​=8×106 Hz​

For a ∼5×1014 Hz line this is a width of about 1.6×10−8 of the frequency: the natural linewidth.

Problem 10 [HW practice · Beiser Ch 3 Problem 1]

A photon and a particle have the same wavelength. Can anything be said about their momenta? Their energies?

Solution

Solution. λ=h/p for both, so their momenta are equal: pγ​=p.

For energies: the photon has Eγ​=pc. The particle has total energy E=p2c2+m2c4​>pc, so the photon's energy is less than the particle's total energy. The particle's kinetic energy is KE=p2c2+m2c4​−mc2<pc (since a2+b2​<a+b), so the photon's energy is greater than the particle's kinetic energy.

KEparticle​<Eγ​<Eparticle​

Problem 11 [HW practice · Beiser Ch 3 Problem 7]

The atomic spacing in rock salt is 0.282 nm. Find the kinetic energy of a neutron with de Broglie wavelength 0.282 nm. Is a relativistic calculation needed?

Solution

Solution. KE=2mp2​=2mn​c2(hc/λ)2​=2(939.6×106 eV)(1240 eV nm/0.282 nm)2​=1.879×109(4397)2​

KE=1.03×10−2 eV​

This is far smaller than mn​c2=940 MeV, so no relativistic calculation is needed. It is of order kB​T at room temperature (0.025 eV): thermal neutrons from a reactor are suited to crystal diffraction.

Problem 12 [HW practice · Beiser Ch 3 Problem 14]

An electron and a proton have the same kinetic energy. Compare the wavelengths and the phase and group velocities of their de Broglie waves.

Solution

Solution. With equal non-relativistic KE, p=2mKE​, so

λp​λe​​=pe​pp​​=me​mp​​​=1836​=42.8​

the electron's wavelength is 43 times longer.

Group velocity is the particle speed, vg​=v=2KE/m​, so vg,e​/vg,p​=mp​/me​​=42.8.

Phase velocity: vp​=ω/k=E/p=c2/v (with E the total energy). So vp,e​/vp,p​=vp​/ve​=1/42.8: the proton's de Broglie waves travel faster. Both exceed c.

Problem 13 [HW practice · Beiser Ch 3 Problem 20]

Show that the group velocity of a wave is vg​=dν/d(1/λ).

Solution

Solution. The group velocity is vg​=dω/dk. With ω=2πν and k=2π/λ, we have dω=2πdν and dk=2πd(1/λ), so

vg​=dkdω​=2πd(1/λ)2πdν​=d(1/λ)dν​​

Problem 14 [HW practice · Beiser Ch 3 Problem 23]

What effect on the scattering angle in the Davisson–Germer experiment does increasing the electron energy have?

Solution

Solution. Increasing the energy increases p, so λ=h/p shrinks. The diffraction maximum for a given order satisfies dsinϕ=nλ (with d the surface-row spacing), so a smaller λ gives a smaller ϕ: the diffracted peak moves to smaller scattering angles, closer to the incident direction (reaching the forward direction for very energetic electrons). Experimentally the peak was strongest at 54 V and ϕ=50∘, consistent with λ=0.165 nm.

Problem 15 [HW practice · Beiser Ch 3 Problem 29]

A proton in a one-dimensional box has energy 400 keV in its first excited state. How wide is the box?

Solution

Solution. First excited state: n=2, E2​=4h2/8mL2=h2/2mL2, so

L=2mE​h​=2(mp​c2)E​hc​=2(938.3)(0.400)​ MeV1240 MeV fm​=27.41240​ fm
L=45 fm=4.5×10−14 m​

This is the size of a large nucleus, and E≪mp​c2, so the non-relativistic formula is valid.

Problem 16 [HW practice · Beiser Ch 3 Problem 33]

The position and momentum of a 1.00 keV electron are determined simultaneously. If its position is located to within 0.100 nm, what is the percentage uncertainty in its momentum?

Solution

Solution. p=2mE​=2(9.11×10−31)(1.60×10−16)​=1.71×10−23 kg m/s. The minimum uncertainty:

Δp≥2Δxℏ​=2(10−10)1.055×10−34​=5.3×10−25 kg m/s
pΔp​=1.71×10−235.3×10−25​=3.1%​

(This is the minimum; with ΔxΔp∼h the figure would be larger.)
