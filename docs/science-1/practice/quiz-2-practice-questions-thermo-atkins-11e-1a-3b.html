Quiz-2 practice questions (thermo, Atkins 11e 1A–3B)

6 min read

Answers are in quiz2-answer-key.md. Attempt first, ideally on paper and without a calculator (as in the exam), then check. Constants: R = 8.314 J/K/mol = 0.08314 L bar/K/mol; k_B = 1.381e-23 J/K; sign convention W = work done ON the system.

Part A: recall (state / write down)

  • A1. State the zeroth law.
  • A2. Write PV in terms of ⟨v²⟩ from kinetic theory, then give ⟨E_k⟩ in terms of T.
  • A3. Write the vdW equation and say what a and b represent.
  • A4. Give C_P,m − C_V,m and γ for a diatomic ideal gas at room temperature.
  • A5. State the Kelvin-Planck and Clausius statements.
  • A6. State the Clausius inequality and the definition of entropy.
  • A7. Give S in terms of microstates.
  • A8. Write the relation between T and V (and P and V) for a reversible adiabatic process.

Part B: understanding (a few lines each)

  • B1. A metal bench feels colder than a wooden one. Which is colder, and what does the zeroth law say?
  • B2. H₂ and O₂ share a box at 300 K. Compare ⟨E_k⟩ and v_rms.
  • B3. For an ideal gas, why is ΔU = nC_VΔT also valid at constant pressure?
  • B4. Explain why an adiabat is steeper than an isotherm on a P-V diagram.
  • B5. A gas expands freely into vacuum in an insulated box. Give Q, W, ΔU and ΔS_gas, ΔS_surr, ΔS_univ. Why does ΔS_gas ≠ Q/T here?
  • B6. Explain the sign of μ_JT for He versus CO₂ at room temperature.
  • B7. Why can heat engines never be 100% efficient even without friction?
  • B8. Water freezes in a freezer. Signs of ΔS_water and ΔS_univ?
  • B9. For a vdW gas, why is π_T = a/V_m² > 0?
  • B10. Why is the Carnot efficiency independent of the working substance?

Part C: derivations (exam-style, concise)

  • C1. Derive P = (N m⟨v_x²⟩)/V for a cubic box and hence ⟨E_k⟩ = 3k_BT/2.
  • C2. Derive v_mp = √(2RT/M) from the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution.
  • C3. Starting from nC_V dT = −P dV, derive PV^γ = const.
  • C4. Derive η_Carnot = 1 − T_C/T_H for an ideal gas.
  • C5. Show that a 100%-efficient engine violating Kelvin-Planck can be built from a Clausius-violating "perfect fridge" plus an ordinary engine.
  • C6. Show that Joule-Thomson expansion is isenthalpic.
  • C7. Show that S = k_B ln W gives ΔS = nR ln 2 for the free expansion that doubles the volume.

Part D: numerical problems

  • D1. 2.00 mol of an ideal monatomic gas is heated from 300 K to 400 K at constant pressure. Find Q, W, ΔU, ΔH.
  • D2. 1.00 mol of an ideal gas is compressed reversibly and isothermally at 300 K from 20 L to 5 L. Find W, Q, ΔS_gas, ΔS_surr, ΔS_univ. (Use ln 4 = 1.386.)
  • D3. 1.00 mol of ideal monatomic gas at 300 K and 1 bar is compressed reversibly and adiabatically to 1/8 of its volume. Find T₂, P₂, and W.
  • D4. Find v_rms of O₂ at 400 K (M = 32 g/mol). By what factor does v_rms of H₂ exceed that of O₂ at the same T?
  • D5. 1.00 mol of N₂ (a = 1.370, b = 0.0387 in L, bar, mol) is held in 0.500 L at 298 K. Compute P_ideal, P_vdW and Z.
  • D6. Use T_I = 2a/(Rb) to estimate the inversion temperature of N₂.
  • D7. A Carnot engine runs between 600 K and 300 K and absorbs 1200 J per cycle. Find η, W, Q_C and ΔS_univ per cycle.
  • D8. Find the maximum COP of a refrigerator working between 270 K (inside) and 300 K (room).
  • D9. An inventor claims an engine that takes 800 J at 500 K, rejects 400 J at 300 K, and does 400 J of work. Is it possible?
  • D10. Compute ΔS for 2.00 kg of water heated from 20 °C to 50 °C (c = 4180 J/kg/K; ln(323.15/293.15) = 0.0974).
  • D11. Mix 1.00 kg of water at 90 °C with 1.00 kg at 10 °C in an insulated vessel. Find the final T and ΔS_univ (use ln 1.0156 ≈ 0.0155 for the argument you get).
  • D12. 600 J of heat flows from a body at 600 K to one at 300 K (both large). Compute ΔS_univ.
  • D13. Boiling 2.00 mol of water at 373.15 K (ΔH_vap = 40.7 kJ/mol): find ΔS_sys and ΔS_surr (reversible).
  • D14. For 6 labelled particles in a two-halved box, how many microstates have 3 on the left? Give S in terms of k_B, and the probability of 3-3 versus 6-0.
  • D15. An Otto engine has compression ratio r = 10 and γ = 1.4. Find η (10^0.4 ≈ 2.51).
  • D16. A constant-volume gas thermometer reads 6.69 kPa at the triple point of water. What does it read at 100 °C, and what temperature corresponds to 8.00 kPa? (Tutorial P1.)
  • D17. 5.00 mol of He in a 1.00 L cylinder at 298 K. Compute P_ideal and P_vdW (a = 0.0346, b = 0.0238), and state which term dominates. (Tutorial P4.)
