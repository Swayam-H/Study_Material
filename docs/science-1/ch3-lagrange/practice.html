Calculus of variations and Lagrangian mechanics

19 min read · 24 practice problems

24 questions with worked answers (tap a solution to open it). Points in brackets, midsem style.

A. State and explain

Problem 1 [Mechanics 2 · Recall · 3 pts · 2026 midsem Q6]

What is Hamilton's principle? Write the mathematical expression for it.

Solution

Solution. Hamilton's principle. Of all the paths a system could take between two configurations in a fixed time interval, the actual one makes the action stationary:

δS=δ∫t1​t2​​L(q,q˙​,t)dt=0,L=T−U

Why it matters. Setting δS=0 gives the Euler–Lagrange equations

dtd​∂q˙​i​∂L​−∂qi​∂L​=0

which are Newton's laws in any coordinates.

Common mistake. The action is stationary, not always a minimum, and the end points (and the time interval) are held fixed.

Problem 2 [Mechanics 2 · Recall · 3 pts · 2026 midsem Q2]

What is the Lagrangian? For L({qᵢ}, {q̇ᵢ}, t), what happens when q₁ does not appear explicitly?

Solution

Solution. The Lagrangian is the kinetic minus the potential energy, expressed in generalised coordinates and velocities:

L(qi​,q˙​i​,t)=T−U

If q1​ does not appear in L (a cyclic or ignorable coordinate), then ∂L/∂q1​=0 and Lagrange's equation reduces to

dtd​∂q˙​1​∂L​=0 ⇒ p1​=∂q˙​1​∂L​=constant

The conjugate momentum is conserved. This is the link between symmetry and conservation laws (Noether): translation symmetry gives linear momentum, rotation symmetry gives angular momentum.

Problem 3 [Mechanics 2 · Recall · 4 pts · 2025 Q6, 2026 Q5]

State the differences between Newtonian and Lagrangian mechanics.

Solution

Solution. | | Newtonian | Lagrangian | |---|---|---| | Basic quantities | vectors: force F, acceleration | scalars: kinetic and potential energy | | Coordinates | Cartesian (or chosen by hand) | any generalised coordinates qi​ | | Constraints | find and balance every constraint force | built into the coordinates (or one multiplier) | | Conservation laws | proved case by case | read off from symmetries (cyclic coordinates) | | Starting principle | three laws | Hamilton's principle | | Friction | handled directly | needs extra generalised forces |

What they share. Both give second-order equations of motion and the same physics. Lagrange is more powerful when there are constraints or non-Cartesian geometry; Newton is simpler for problems with non-conservative forces.

Problem 4 [Mechanics 2 · Recall · 2 pts]

What are generalised coordinates and degrees of freedom? Give the number for a double pendulum in a plane.

Solution

Solution. Generalised coordinates q1​,…,qn​ are independent quantities that completely fix the configuration of the system (lengths, angles, ...). The number of degrees of freedom is the number of independent coordinates needed.

DOF=(number of Cartesian coordinates)−(number of independent constraints)

Double pendulum in a plane. Two particles, so 4 Cartesian coordinates (x1​,y1​,x2​,y2​), minus 2 rod-length constraints:

2 degrees of freedom​(θ1​,θ2​)

Common mistake. In 3-D the double pendulum has 4 degrees of freedom, not 2.

Problem 5 [Mechanics 2 · Recall · 2 pts]

Why do constraint forces not appear in Lagrange's equations? When would you use a Lagrange multiplier?

Solution

Solution. Why constraint forces drop out. An ideal constraint force (normal force, tension in a rigid rod) is perpendicular to every displacement the constraint allows, so it does no work in such a displacement:

Fconstraint​⋅δr=0

Hamilton's principle varies the path only through allowed displacements, so these forces never appear.

When to use a Lagrange multiplier.

  • You want the constraint force itself (the normal force on a sphere, friction in rolling).
  • The constraint is awkward to build into the coordinates, for example rolling y−Rθ=0 or staying on a sphere r−R=0.

Then L gets an extra term and the equations become dtd​∂q˙​∂L​−∂q∂L​=λ∂q∂f​, where λ∂f/∂q is the generalised constraint force.

Problem 6 [Mechanics 2 · Recall · 3 pts]

Explain how time, translation and rotation symmetry lead to conservation of energy, momentum and angular momentum.

Solution

Solution. Noether's idea. A symmetry of L gives a conserved quantity.

symmetry L unchanged under conserved
time translation t→t+ϵ (∂L/∂t=0) energy, H=∑q˙​p−L
space translation q→q+ϵ (q cyclic) momentum, p=∂L/∂q˙​
rotation θ→θ+ϵ (θ cyclic) angular momentum, r×p

Why a cyclic coordinate works. If ∂L/∂q=0, Lagrange's equation gives dtd​∂q˙​∂L​=0, so pq​ is constant.

Problem 7 [Mechanics 2 · Recall · 2 pts]

How is calculus-of-variations optimisation different from ordinary function optimisation?

Solution

Solution. Ordinary optimisation. Find a number x (or finitely many) that makes a function f(x) stationary, by solving the algebraic equation f′(x)=0.

Calculus of variations. Find a whole function y(x) that makes a functional

J[y]=∫f(y,y′;x)dx

stationary. The unknown lives in an infinite-dimensional space, and the stationarity condition is a differential equation (the Euler equation), not an algebraic one:

∂y∂f​−dxd​∂y′∂f​=0

Examples. Shortest path (straight line), fastest path (brachistochrone), Fermat's principle (Snell's law), and all of Lagrangian mechanics.

Problem 8 [Mechanics 2 · Derivation · 4 pts]

Derive the Euler equation for J = ∫f(y, y′; x) dx, and its second form when f has no explicit x.

Solution

Solution. Setup. Find y(x) that makes J=∫x1​x2​​f(y,y′;x)dx stationary, with the end points fixed.

Vary the path. y→y+αη(x) with η(x1​)=η(x2​)=0:

dαdJ​​α=0​=∫(∂y∂f​η+∂y′∂f​η′)dx=0

Integrate the second term by parts. The boundary term [∂y′∂f​η] vanishes since η=0 at the ends:

∫(∂y∂f​−dxd​∂y′∂f​)ηdx=0

This holds for every η, so

∂y∂f​−dxd​∂y′∂f​=0​

Second form (no explicit x). Expand

dxdf​=y′fy​+y′′fy′​+fx​=dxd​(y′fy′​)+y′(fy​−dxd​fy′​)+fx​

The bracket is zero by Euler's equation. If fx​=0:

f−y′∂y′∂f​=constant​

Problem 9 [Mechanics 2 · Derivation · 3 pts · 2026 midsem Q4]

Show that Fermat's principle of least time leads to Snell's law.

Solution

Solution. Setup. A is a height a above the interface, B a depth b below, horizontal separation d. Light crosses at x (measured from below A), with speeds v1​,v2​.

t(x)=v1​a2+x2​​+v2​b2+(d−x)2​​

Minimise.

dxdt​=v1​a2+x2​x​−v2​b2+(d−x)2​d−x​=v1​sinθ1​​−v2​sinθ2​​=0

where θ1​,θ2​ are the angles to the normal. With v=c/n:

n1​sinθ1​=n2​sinθ2​​

Check. Going into a slower medium (n2​>n1​), θ2​<θ1​: the ray bends toward the normal.

Problem 10 [Mechanics 2 · Derivation · 2 pts]

Show that the shortest path between two points in a plane is a straight line.

Solution

Solution. Path length. J=∫ds=∫1+y′2​dx, so f=1+y′2​.

Euler's equation. f has no explicit y, so ∂f/∂y=0 and

∂y′∂f​=1+y′2​y′​=constant

This forces $y' = $ constant, so y=mx+c: a straight line.

Problem 11 [Mechanics 2 · Derivation · 4 pts · MT E6.2]

Set up the brachistochrone integral and show the curve satisfies y′²/[x(1 + y′²)] = constant.

Solution

Solution. Setup. Take x measured downward from the start, released from rest. Energy conservation gives the speed after falling x:

v=2gx​

Time of descent.

T=∫vds​=∫2gx​1+y′2​​dx,f=2gx1+y′2​​

Euler. f has no explicit y, so $\partial f/\partial y' = $ const:

2gx(1+y′2)​y′​=const⇒x(1+y′2)y′2​=2a1​​

(the constant is written 1/2a by convention). Solving this gives the cycloid x=a(1−cosθ), y=a(θ−sinθ).

Problem 12 [Mechanics 2 · Derivation · 8 pts · 2026 midsem Q7] (hard)

A double pendulum has point masses m₁, m₂ on massless rods l₁, l₂. Identify the generalised coordinates and degrees of freedom, construct L, and obtain the equations of motion.

Solution

Solution. Coordinates. Two angles θ1​,θ2​ from the vertical: 2 degrees of freedom.

Positions.

x1​=l1​sinθ1​,y1​=−l1​cosθ1​;x2​=x1​+l2​sinθ2​,y2​=y1​−l2​cosθ2​

Kinetic energy. v22​=l12​θ˙12​+l22​θ˙22​+2l1​l2​θ˙1​θ˙2​cos(θ1​−θ2​), so

T=21​(m1​+m2​)l12​θ˙12​+21​m2​l22​θ˙22​+m2​l1​l2​θ˙1​θ˙2​cos(θ1​−θ2​)
U=−(m1​+m2​)gl1​cosθ1​−m2​gl2​cosθ2​,L=T−U

Lagrange's equations.

θ1​: (m1​+m2​)l12​θ¨1​+m2​l1​l2​θ¨2​cos(θ1​−θ2​)+m2​l1​l2​θ˙22​sin(θ1​−θ2​)+(m1​+m2​)gl1​sinθ1​=0
θ2​: m2​l22​θ¨2​+m2​l1​l2​θ¨1​cos(θ1​−θ2​)−m2​l1​l2​θ˙12​sin(θ1​−θ2​)+m2​gl2​sinθ2​=0

Check. For small angles with m2​→0 the first equation reduces to a simple pendulum, θ¨1​=−(g/l1​)θ1​ ✓.

Problem 13 [Mechanics 2 · Derivation · 5 pts · 2025 midsem Q12]

Repeat Q12 for two uniform rods of masses m₁, m₂ hinged together (no point masses). Only write L.

Solution

Solution. Rod 1 (pivot at its end): I=31​m1​l12​, so

T1​=21​⋅31​m1​l12​θ˙12​=61​m1​l12​θ˙12​

Rod 2. Centre-of-mass velocity is the sum of l1​θ˙1​ (the hinge) and 21​l2​θ˙2​ (about the hinge); the spin is about the rod's own centre with Icm​=121​m2​l22​:

T2​=21​m2​[l12​θ˙12​+41​l22​θ˙22​+l1​l2​θ˙1​θ˙2​cos(θ1​−θ2​)]+241​m2​l22​θ˙22​

Potential. Heights of the two centres of mass:

U=−m1​g2l1​​cosθ1​−m2​g(l1​cosθ1​+2l2​​cosθ2​)
L=T1​+T2​−U​

Common mistake. Forgetting the extra rotational term 241​m2​l22​θ˙22​ for rod 2.

Problem 14 [Mechanics 2 · Derivation · 4 pts · MT E7.4]

A particle moves on the inside of a cone of half-angle α under gravity. Find L and the equations of motion. What is conserved?

Solution

Solution. Constraint. On the cone, z=rcotα, so z˙=r˙cotα and

v2=r˙2+r2θ˙2+z˙2=r˙2csc2α+r2θ˙2
L=21​m(r˙2csc2α+r2θ˙2)−mgrcotα

Equations.

  • θ is cyclic: dtd​(mr2θ˙)=0⇒mr2θ˙=ℓ​ (angular momentum about the axis).
  • r: mr¨csc2α=mrθ˙2−mgcotα, so
r¨=rθ˙2sin2α−gsinαcosα​

Conserved. ℓ (axial symmetry) and the energy T+U (no explicit time).

Problem 15 [Mechanics 2 · Derivation · 3 pts · MT P7.12, practice P19]

A block slides on a smooth incline whose angle grows as θ = αt. Find the equation of motion. Is energy conserved?

Solution

Solution. Coordinate. q = distance down the incline, whose angle is θ=αt (driven). The block sits at (qcosαt, qsinαt), so its height is qsinαt and the incline's rotation adds a transverse speed qα:

v2=q˙​2+q2α2
L=21​m(q˙​2+α2q2)−mgqsinαt

Equation. mq¨​=mα2q−mgsinαt:

q¨​=α2q−gsinαt​

Energy. L depends explicitly on t (the incline is driven from outside), so ∂L/∂t=0 and H is not conserved.

Problem 16 [Mechanics 2 · Derivation · 4 pts · MT E7.9]

A disk rolls without slipping down an incline of angle α. Using a Lagrange multiplier, find its acceleration and the friction force.

Solution

Solution. Coordinates. y is the distance down the slope and θ the rotation angle; rolling without slipping means

f=y−Rθ=0
L=21​My˙​2+21​Iθ˙2+Mgysinα

Lagrange with a multiplier λ: dtd​∂q˙​∂L​−∂q∂L​=λ∂q∂f​:

y: My¨​−Mgsinα=λ,θ: Iθ¨=−λR

Eliminate. With θ¨=y¨​/R: λ=−Iy¨​/R2, so

My¨​+R2I​y¨​=Mgsinα⇒y¨​=1+I/MR2gsinα​​

Uniform disk (I=21​MR2): y¨​=32​gsinα, and the friction force is

∣λ∣=R2I​y¨​=31​Mgsinα​(up the slope)

Check. Frictionless sliding would give gsinα; rolling is slower because energy also goes into rotation.

Problem 17 [Mechanics 2 · Derivation · 3 pts · practice P20]

A pendulum of length b hangs from a support accelerating horizontally at a. Find the equation of motion and the period of small oscillations.

Solution

Solution. Coordinates. Let the support move with acceleration a, and θ the angle of the string from the vertical. Position of the bob:

x=21​at2+bsinθ,y=−bcosθ
L=21​mb2θ˙2+mbatθ˙cosθ+21​ma2t2+mgbcosθ

Lagrange's equation (dropping terms that do not involve θ):

bθ¨=−gsinθ−acosθ

Equilibrium. tanθ0​=−a/g: the bob hangs back from the acceleration, at an angle tan−1(a/g).

Small oscillations about it behave like a pendulum in an effective gravity geff​=g2+a2​:

T=2πg2+a2​b​​​

Check. a=0 gives 2πb/g​ ✓.

Problem 18 [Mechanics 2 · Derivation · 3 pts · practice P22]

A rope of mass m and length b lies on a smooth table with length z hanging over the edge. Find Lagrange's equation.

Solution

Solution. Coordinate. z = length hanging. The whole rope moves with speed z˙.

T=21​mz˙2

Potential. The hanging part has mass mz/b and its centre is z/2 below the table:

U=−bmz​g2z​=−2bmgz2​
L=21​mz˙2+2bmgz2​

Lagrange. mz¨=mgz/b:

z¨=bg​z​

The solution grows exponentially, z∝eg/b​t (a runaway: more hanging means more pull).

Problem 19 [Mechanics 2 · Derivation · 3 pts · practice P17]

A particle moves in a plane under an attractive central force f = −Ar^(α−1). Find the Lagrange equations. Are angular momentum and energy conserved?

Solution

Solution. Potential. From f=−Arα−1=−dU/dr, with U(0)=0:

U=αA​rα
L=21​m(r˙2+r2θ˙2)−αA​rα

Equations.

  • θ: dtd​(mr2θ˙)=0, so angular momentum is conserved.
  • r: mr¨−mrθ˙2+Arα−1=0.

Energy. L has no explicit time dependence, so T+U is conserved too.

Problem 20 [Mechanics 2 · Derivation · 4 pts · slides HW]

Show that if L is unchanged by any rotation δr = δθ × r, then r × p is conserved.

Solution

Solution. Infinitesimal rotation. δr=δθ×r and δv=δθ×v.

δL=∂r∂L​⋅δr+∂v∂L​⋅δv=p˙​⋅(δθ×r)+p⋅(δθ×v)

using Lagrange's equation ∂L/∂r=p˙​.

Cyclic permutation of the triple product (a⋅(b×c)=b⋅(c×a)):

δL=δθ⋅(r×p˙​+v×p)=δθ⋅dtd​(r×p)

(the term v×p is zero). If L is rotation-invariant, δL=0 for every δθ, so r×p is constant.

Problem 21 [Mechanics 2 · Derivation · 3 pts]

Show that when ∂L/∂t = 0, H = Σq̇p − L is conserved, and that it equals T + U for L = ½mv² − U(x).

Solution

Solution. Time derivative of L.

dtdL​=∑∂q∂L​q˙​+∑∂q˙​∂L​q¨​+∂t∂L​

Use Lagrange's equation ∂L/∂q=p˙​. The first two sums combine into dtd​∑pq˙​:

dtd​(∑pq˙​−L)=−∂t∂L​

So H=∑pq˙​−L is conserved when ∂L/∂t=0.

For L=21​mv2−U(x). p=mv, so ∑pq˙​=mv2:

H=mv2−21​mv2+U=21​mv2+U=T+U

Problem 22 [Mechanics 2 · Derivation · 5 pts · practice P30]

A particle slides from rest at the top of a smooth sphere of radius R. Using a multiplier for r = R, find the normal force and the angle at which it leaves.

Solution

Solution. Coordinates. r,θ with θ from the vertical; constraint f=r−R=0.

L=21​m(r˙2+r2θ˙2)−mgrcosθ

r-equation with multiplier (λ is the outward constraint force): mr¨−mrθ˙2+mgcosθ=λ.

On the surface (r=R, r¨=0) the normal force is

N=λ=mgcosθ−mRθ˙2

Energy (from rest at the top): 21​mR2θ˙2=mgR(1−cosθ)⇒Rθ˙2=2g(1−cosθ).

N=mg(3cosθ−2)​

Leaves the surface when N=0:

cosθ=32​,θ≈48∘​

C. Short numericals

Problem 23 [Mechanics 2 · Numerical · 2 pts]

Find the acceleration of a uniform hoop (I = MR²) and a uniform disk rolling down a 30° incline.

Solution

Solution. Idea. For a body rolling without slipping, a=1+I/MR2gsinα​. With sin30∘=21​:

Hoop (I=MR2):a=1+1g/2​=4g​​
Uniform disk (I=21​MR2):a=1+21​g/2​=3g​​

The disk, with less of its mass at the rim, accelerates faster.

Problem 24 [Mechanics 2 · Numerical · 2 pts · practice P18a]

Two blocks of mass M are joined by a light string; one is on a smooth table, the other hangs over a pulley. Find the acceleration.
