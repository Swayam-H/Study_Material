Thermodynamics definitions

32 min read

This sheet goes with the formula sheet (sheet 6). It lists every term with a precise definition, then the laws stated in exam-ready form.

  • Tags: [A] = Atkins only, [S] = slides only, [Z] = Zemansky.
  • Convention: ΔU=q+w, with q and w counted as energy into the system.

1A Systems, state, temperature, perfect gas

Systems and boundaries

Term Definition Notes
system The part of the universe chosen for study. Choosing it well often makes a problem easy.
surroundings Everything outside the system, where measurements are made.
boundary What separates the system from its surroundings. It may be real or imaginary, fixed or movable.
universe The system together with its surroundings. ΔSuniv​=ΔSsys​+ΔSsur​
open system A system that can exchange both matter and energy with its surroundings. Example: a pot with no lid.
closed system A system that can exchange energy but not matter. Example: a sealed flask or piston.
isolated system A system that can exchange neither matter nor energy. Example: an ideal thermos. ΔU=0.
diathermic wall A boundary that allows energy to pass as heat.
adiabatic wall A boundary that does not allow energy to pass as heat.

Properties and state

Term Definition Notes
intensive property A property that does not depend on the amount of substance. p, T, density, Vm​
extensive property A property proportional to the amount of substance. V, n, m, U, H, S, C
molar quantity An extensive quantity divided by the amount: Xm​=X/n. It is intensive.
specific quantity An extensive quantity divided by the mass. e.g. specific heat capacity (J K⁻¹ g⁻¹)
state The condition of a system, fixed by the values of its state variables.
state variable / state function A property whose value depends only on the current state, not on how the state was reached. U, H, S, p, V, T. ∮dX=0.
path function A quantity whose value depends on the route taken between two states. q, w. A body does not "contain" heat or work.
equation of state A relation between the state variables of a substance. p=f(T,V,n), e.g. pV=nRT

Equilibrium

Term Definition Notes
thermodynamic equilibrium The macroscopic properties no longer change with time and are uniform throughout. It requires thermal, mechanical and chemical equilibrium.
thermal equilibrium No change of state occurs when two bodies are in contact through a diathermic wall. They then have the same temperature.
mechanical equilibrium There is no unbalanced force. Equal pressure on both sides of a movable wall.

Pressure and temperature

Term Definition Notes
pressure Force divided by the area it acts on: p=F/A. Pa = N m⁻²; 1 bar = 10⁵ Pa; 1 atm = 101 325 Pa
standard pressure p° Exactly 1 bar.
temperature The property that tells whether two bodies in thermal contact will exchange energy as heat; heat flows from higher to lower T. Kinetic theory: a measure of the average translational KE.
thermometer A device whose thermometric property (length of a liquid column, gas pressure, resistance) changes with T. It works because of the zeroth law.
triple point of water The single T, p at which ice, liquid water and vapour coexist. T3​ = 273.16 K exactly (fixed point of the Kelvin scale).
Celsius scale θ θ/∘C=T/K−273.15.
thermodynamic (Kelvin) scale The absolute scale with T=0 at absolute zero, defined by TC​/TH​=QC​/QH​ for a reversible engine (3A.3). It agrees with the perfect-gas scale.
absolute zero T=0 K (−273.15 °C). Where all gases' p extrapolates to zero at constant V. It is unattainable.

Gases and gas laws

Term Definition Notes
perfect (ideal) gas A gas that obeys pV=nRT exactly at all conditions: no interactions and negligible molecular size. Real gases approach it as p→0.
limiting law A law that is strictly true only in a limit, here p→0. The perfect gas law is one.
Boyle's law At constant n and T, pV = constant.
Charles's law At constant n and p, V∝T. At constant n and V, p∝T.
Avogadro's principle At the same T and p, equal volumes of gases contain equal numbers of molecules: V∝n.
amount of substance n Measured in moles; 1 mol contains NA​ entities. NA​=6.022×1023 mol⁻¹
molar mass M Mass per amount of substance. Use kg mol⁻¹ in kinetic formulas.
SATP 298.15 K and 1 bar, where Vm​ = 24.79 L mol⁻¹. STP: 273.15 K and 1 atm, where Vm​ = 22.41 L mol⁻¹.
mole fraction xJ​ xJ​=nJ​/n. ∑J​xJ​=1 [A]
partial pressure pJ​ pJ​=xJ​p: the pressure component J would exert if it alone occupied the container. [A]
Dalton's law The pressure of a mixture of perfect gases is the sum of the partial pressures. Exact only for perfect gases. [A]

1B Kinetic model

Term Definition Notes
kinetic model of gases A gas is a collection of molecules of mass m in ceaseless random motion. Their size is negligible compared with the distances they travel, and they interact only through brief elastic collisions.
elastic collision A collision in which the total translational kinetic energy is conserved.
root-mean-square speed vrms​ The square root of the mean of the squared speeds, ⟨v2⟩​=3RT/M​.
mean speed vmean​ The average speed, ∫0∞​vf(v)dv=8RT/πM​.
most probable speed vmp​ The speed at the peak of f(v), 2RT/M​.
mean relative speed vrel​ The mean speed at which one molecule approaches another, 2​vmean​ for identical molecules. [A]
Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution f(v), where f(v)dv is the fraction of molecules with speeds between v and v+dv. Normalised: ∫0∞​fdv=1
collision diameter d The separation of two molecular centres at contact. [A]
collision cross-section σ The target area σ=πd2 within which two molecules collide. [A]
collision frequency z The number of collisions one molecule makes per unit time, z=σvrel​N/V. s⁻¹ [A]
mean free path λ The average distance a molecule travels between collisions, λ=vrel​/z=kB​T/σp. [A]
degree of freedom An independent way a molecule can store energy (translation, rotation, vibration).
equipartition theorem At thermal equilibrium, each quadratic term in a molecule's energy has an average value of 21​kB​T. Valid when the levels are closely spaced (classical); vibrations are often frozen.
escape speed The minimum speed needed to leave a planet's gravity: 2gR​. 11.2 km/s for Earth

1C Real gases

Term Definition Notes
real gas A gas whose molecules interact and have finite size, so it deviates from pV=nRT. Deviations are largest at high p and low T.
repulsive interaction A short-range force that dominates when molecules are nearly in contact. It makes the gas harder to compress than a perfect gas, so Z>1.
attractive interaction A longer-range force at intermediate separations. It makes the gas easier to compress, so Z<1.
compression factor Z Z=pVm​/RT, the ratio of the molar volume to that of a perfect gas at the same T and p. Z=1 for a perfect gas
virial equation An expansion of pVm​/RT in powers of 1/Vm​ (or p): 1+B/Vm​+C/Vm2​+⋯. [A]
virial coefficients B, C, … The temperature-dependent coefficients of that expansion. B is the second virial coefficient. [A]
Boyle temperature TB​ The temperature at which B=0, so the gas behaves almost perfectly over a range of p. vdW: TB​=a/Rb [A]
van der Waals equation (p+a/Vm2​)(Vm​−b)=RT, a model equation of state for real gases.
vdW parameter a Measures the strength of attractions; reduces the pressure by a/Vm2​. L² bar mol⁻²
vdW parameter b The excluded volume per mole, i.e. the volume the molecules themselves occupy. L mol⁻¹
critical point The state (Tc​, pc​, Vc​) at which liquid and vapour become indistinguishable. [A]
critical temperature Tc​ Above Tc​ a gas cannot be liquefied by pressure alone. [A]
supercritical fluid Matter above both Tc​ and pc​, which is a single dense fluid phase. [A]
van der Waals loops Unphysical oscillations of vdW isotherms below Tc​. [A]
Maxwell construction Replacing a vdW loop by a horizontal line that encloses equal areas above and below it. The line represents liquid–vapour coexistence. [A]
reduced variables pr​=p/pc​, Tr​=T/Tc​, Vr​=Vm​/Vc​. [A]
principle of corresponding states Real gases at the same reduced volume and reduced temperature exert the same reduced pressure (they have about the same Z). An approximation; best for spherical molecules. [A]

2A Work, heat, internal energy

Energy, work and heat

Term Definition Notes
energy The capacity to do work. J
work Energy transferred by motion against an opposing force. In principle it could raise a weight in the surroundings. Molecular view: transfer that uses organised motion.
heat Energy transferred because of a temperature difference between the system and its surroundings. Molecular view: transfer that uses disorderly thermal motion. Energy in transit, not contained.
exothermic process A process that releases energy as heat to the surroundings (q<0). e.g. combustion
endothermic process A process that absorbs energy as heat (q>0). e.g. vaporisation
internal energy U The total energy of a system: the kinetic and potential energy of its molecules, from translation, rotation, vibration, interactions and bonds. State function, extensive. Only changes ΔU are measurable.
first law The internal energy of an isolated system is constant. For a closed system, ΔU=q+w. Energy is conserved.
perpetual motion machine of the first kind A device that produces work without consuming energy. The first law forbids it.

Expansion work

Term Definition Notes
expansion work Work arising from a change in volume, dw=−pex​dV.
non-expansion (additional) work Any work other than expansion work: electrical, surface, extension, chemical. dwadd​ [A]
external pressure pex​ The pressure the surroundings exert on the boundary. It is what enters dw=−pex​dV.
free expansion Expansion against zero external pressure (into a vacuum), so w=0.
indicator diagram A plot of p against V for a process. The area under the curve is ∣w∣.
quasi-static process A process slow enough that the system passes through a continuous sequence of equilibrium states, so it can be drawn as a curve on a p–V diagram. [S]
reversible process A change that can be reversed by an infinitesimal change in a variable, leaving both the system and the surroundings as they were. The system stays in equilibrium with its surroundings throughout. Quasi-static, with no dissipation. It gives the maximum work.
irreversible process Any real process that cannot be undone without leaving a change in the surroundings. Friction, finite ΔT, free expansion, mixing

Processes

Term Definition Notes
isothermal At constant temperature.
isobaric At constant pressure.
isochoric At constant volume.
adiabatic With no heat transfer, q=0.
cyclic process A process that returns the system to its initial state, so Δ(any state function)=0.

Other kinds of work

Term Definition Notes
generalised force / displacement Work in any form is dw=YdX: an intensive force Y times the change of an extensive displacement X. [S][Z]
surface tension γ The work needed to increase a surface's area by unit amount, dw=γdA. N m⁻¹ = J m⁻²
isothermal Young's modulus Y=(L/A)(∂F/∂L)T​, the stiffness of a wire. [Z]
emf E The potential difference of a cell on open circuit. Electrical work is EdZ. [S]
Curie's law For a paramagnet, M=CC​H/T. [S][Z]

Heat capacity and calorimetry

Term Definition Notes
heat capacity C The heat needed to raise the temperature by one kelvin, C=dq/dT. J K⁻¹. It depends on the path, so state the conditions.
CV​ Heat capacity at constant volume, (∂U/∂T)V​.
molar heat capacity Cm​=C/n. J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹
specific heat capacity Cs​=C/m. Water: 4.18 J K⁻¹ g⁻¹
calorimetry The measurement of heat transferred during a process. [A]
calorimeter constant Ccal​ The heat capacity of a calorimeter, found from a known heat input: Ccal​=q/ΔT. [A]
bomb calorimeter A constant-volume calorimeter, so qV​=ΔU. [A]

2B Enthalpy

Term Definition Notes
enthalpy H H=U+pV. State function. At constant p with no additional work, ΔH=qp​.
Cp​ Heat capacity at constant pressure, (∂H/∂T)p​. Cp​>CV​ because part of the heat does expansion work.
heat capacity ratio γ γ=Cp​/CV​. 5/3 for a monatomic gas, 7/5 for a diatomic gas
isobaric calorimeter A constant-pressure calorimeter, which measures ΔH. e.g. a coffee-cup calorimeter [A]

2C Thermochemistry [A]

Standard states and transitions

Term Definition Notes
thermochemistry The study of the energy transferred as heat in physical and chemical changes.
standard state The pure substance at exactly 1 bar, at the specified temperature. Tables use 298.15 K.
standard enthalpy change ΔH° The enthalpy change when reactants in their standard states become products in their standard states. kJ mol⁻¹
standard enthalpy of transition Δtrs​H° The standard enthalpy change accompanying a change of physical state. Vaporisation, fusion, sublimation
enthalpy of vaporisation / fusion / sublimation The enthalpy change per mole for liquid → gas / solid → liquid / solid → gas. Δsub​H=Δfus​H+Δvap​H

Reactions

Term Definition Notes
stoichiometric number ν The coefficient of a species in a balanced equation. Products positive, reactants negative, in sums like ∑νJ​XJ​.
thermochemical equation A balanced chemical equation together with its Δr​H.
reaction enthalpy Δr​H The change in enthalpy per mole of reaction as written.
standard enthalpy of combustion Δc​H° The standard reaction enthalpy for complete oxidation of a compound to CO₂(g) and H₂O(l) (and N₂(g) if N is present). Always negative
reference state The most stable state of an element at the specified T and 1 bar. e.g. C(graphite), O₂(g), Hg(l)
standard enthalpy of formation Δf​H° The standard reaction enthalpy for forming 1 mol of a compound from its elements in their reference states. Δf​H°=0 for elements in their reference states

Laws and techniques

Term Definition Notes
Hess's law The standard enthalpy of an overall reaction is the sum of the standard enthalpies of the individual reactions it can be divided into. It follows from H being a state function.
Kirchhoff's law Δr​H°(T2​)=Δr​H°(T1​)+∫T1​T2​​Δr​Cp​°dT
differential scanning calorimetry (DSC) Sample and reference are heated at the same rate. The extra power the sample needs gives its excess heat capacity, and the area under the peak gives the transition enthalpy.
isothermal titration calorimetry (ITC) Measures the heat released or absorbed as one reactant is titrated into another at constant T.

2D State functions and exact differentials

Term Definition Notes
exact differential A differential df whose integral depends only on its end points: ∫AB​df=fB​−fA​, ∮df=0. Test: ∂M/∂y=∂N/∂x. dU, dH, dS.
inexact differential A differential whose integral depends on the path. dq, dw
internal pressure πT​ πT​=(∂U/∂V)T​, the change in internal energy with volume at constant T. Units of pressure. 0 for a perfect gas; a/Vm2​ for vdW.
Joule experiment Free expansion of a gas into a vacuum inside a water bath, looking for ΔT. None was seen, because the bath swamped it, so πT​ appeared to be 0.
thermodynamic equation of state πT​=T(∂p/∂T)V​−p. Derived in 3E
expansion coefficient α α=(1/V)(∂V/∂T)p​, the fractional change in volume per unit rise in T. K⁻¹; 1/T for a perfect gas [A]
isothermal compressibility κT​ κT​=−(1/V)(∂V/∂p)T​, the fractional decrease in volume per unit increase in p. Pa⁻¹; 1/p for a perfect gas [A]
Joule–Thomson effect The change in temperature of a gas expanding steadily through a throttle from high to low pressure.
throttling Steady flow through a porous plug or valve in an insulated pipe.
isenthalpic At constant enthalpy. JT expansion is isenthalpic.
Joule–Thomson coefficient μ μ=(∂T/∂p)H​. K bar⁻¹. μ>0 means the gas cools on expansion.
isothermal JT coefficient μT​ μT​=(∂H/∂p)T​=−Cp​μ.
inversion temperature TI​ The temperature at which μ changes sign. vdW: TI​=2a/Rb
inversion curve The boundary in p–T space inside which μ>0 (cooling).
Linde refrigerator A liquefier that repeatedly throttles a compressed gas and uses the cold gas to pre-cool the incoming gas.

2E Adiabatic changes

Term Definition Notes
adiabatic change A change with q=0, so ΔU=wad​.
reversible adiabatic change An adiabatic change carried out reversibly. For a perfect gas, pVγ = const and ΔS=0 (isentropic).
isotherm A curve of constant T on a p–V diagram.
adiabat A curve followed in a reversible adiabatic change. It is steeper than an isotherm because γ>1.
isentropic At constant entropy. A reversible adiabatic process is isentropic.

3A Second law, engines, entropy

Spontaneity and the second law

Term Definition Notes
spontaneous change A change that tends to occur without needing to be driven by work from outside. It says nothing about rate.
second law The entropy of an isolated system increases in the course of a spontaneous change: ΔStot​>0. Equivalent statements are below.
dispersal of energy The direction of spontaneous change is the one that leads to a more chaotic dispersal of the total energy of an isolated system. [A]
perpetual motion machine of the second kind A cyclic device that converts heat from a single reservoir entirely into work. The second law forbids it.
arrow of time Spontaneous processes are those that increase Suniv​. This gives time a direction. [S]

Engines

Term Definition Notes
heat engine A cyclic device that absorbs heat from a hot source, converts part of it into work, and rejects the rest to a cold sink.
hot source / cold sink (reservoir) A body with a heat capacity so large that its temperature stays constant as heat flows in or out.
efficiency η Work output divided by heat absorbed from the hot source: η=W/QH​. η<1 always
refrigerator An engine run backwards, using work to move heat from cold to hot. Its purpose is to cool.
heat pump The same device, where the purpose is to heat the hot side.
coefficient of performance (COP) Fridge: QC​/W. Heat pump: QH​/W. It can exceed 1. [S]

Carnot cycle and temperature

Term Definition Notes
Carnot cycle A reversible cycle of four stages: isothermal expansion at TH​, adiabatic expansion, isothermal compression at TC​, adiabatic compression.
Carnot engine A reversible engine operating on a Carnot cycle. η=1−TC​/TH​
Carnot's theorem No engine operating between two reservoirs is more efficient than a reversible engine, and all reversible engines between the same two reservoirs have the same efficiency, whatever the working substance.
thermodynamic temperature The temperature defined by TC​/TH​=QC​/QH​ for a reversible engine, with T3​ = 273.16 K. Independent of any substance
Otto cycle The ideal petrol-engine cycle: adiabatic compression, isochoric heating, adiabatic expansion, isochoric cooling. [S]
compression ratio r r=Vmax​/Vmin​. ηOtto​=1−r1−γ [S]

Entropy

Term Definition Notes
entropy S (thermodynamic) A state function defined by dS=dqrev​/T. J K⁻¹, extensive
integrating factor A factor that turns an inexact differential into an exact one. 1/T does this for dqrev​.
entropy of the surroundings ΔSsur​=qsur​/Tsur​, valid for any process, because the surroundings act as a reservoir.
Clausius inequality dS≥dq/T, equivalently ∮dq/T≤0, with equality only for reversible changes.
principle of entropy increase For an isolated system, ΔS≥0. At equilibrium, S is a maximum.

Statistical entropy

Term Definition Notes
microstate A complete specification of the state of every particle: which particle is where, or which energy level each occupies.
macrostate The state described by macroscopic variables, e.g. how many particles are in each half of a box. Many microstates give one macrostate.
weight W The number of microstates corresponding to a given macrostate. W=N!/[n!(N−n)!] for two halves
Boltzmann formula S=kB​lnW, the statistical definition of entropy. The logarithm makes S additive.
statistical entropy Entropy as a measure of the number of ways the energy and matter of a system can be arranged consistent with its macrostate. Better phrasing than "disorder".

3B Entropy changes

Term Definition Notes
entropy of transition Δtrs​S The entropy change for a phase transition at its transition temperature: Δtrs​H/Ttrs​. Positive for melting and vaporisation
transition temperature Ttrs​ The temperature at which two phases are in equilibrium at the given pressure. e.g. normal boiling point at 1 atm
Trouton's rule Many liquids have about the same standard entropy of vaporisation, around 85 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Exceptions: H-bonded liquids such as water (109). [A]
composite process A change in which several variables change at once. Find ΔS by splitting it into reversible steps between the same end states. ΔS is a state function. [A]

Laws and principles, stated for exams

Law Statement
Zeroth law If A is in thermal equilibrium with B, and B is in thermal equilibrium with C, then C is also in thermal equilibrium with A.
First law The internal energy of an isolated system is constant. For a closed system, ΔU=q+w, where U is a state function.
Second law (entropy) The entropy of an isolated system increases in a spontaneous change: ΔStot​>0. It is zero only for a reversible change.
Second law (Kelvin–Planck) No process is possible in which the sole result is the absorption of heat from a reservoir and its complete conversion into work. Equivalently, no cyclic engine works with a single reservoir.
Second law (Clausius) Heat does not flow spontaneously from a colder body to a hotter one. No cyclic device can transfer heat from cold to hot with no other effect.
Carnot's theorem No engine operating between two given reservoirs can be more efficient than a reversible one. All reversible engines between them have efficiency 1−TC​/TH​.
Clausius inequality For any cycle, ∮dq/T≤0, with equality for a reversible cycle. Equivalently, dS≥dq/T.
Principle of entropy increase The entropy of an isolated system never decreases.
Boyle's law At fixed n and T, p∝1/V.
Charles's law At fixed n and p, V∝T.
Avogadro's principle Equal volumes of gases at the same T and p contain the same number of molecules.
Dalton's law The pressure of a mixture of perfect gases is the sum of their partial pressures. [A]
Equipartition theorem For a sample at thermal equilibrium, the average value of each quadratic contribution to the energy is 21​kB​T.
Principle of corresponding states Real gases at the same reduced volume and reduced temperature exert the same reduced pressure. [A]
Hess's law The standard enthalpy of an overall reaction is the sum of the standard enthalpies of the reactions it can be divided into. [A]
Kirchhoff's law The change of a reaction enthalpy with temperature is given by dΔr​H°/dT=Δr​Cp​°. [A]
Trouton's rule Δvap​S°≈85 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ for many liquids at their normal boiling points. [A]

Easily confused pairs

Pair Difference
heat vs temperature Heat is energy transferred because of ΔT. Temperature is the property that decides which way heat flows.
heat vs internal energy A hot body has more internal energy, not "more heat".
quasi-static vs reversible Reversible = quasi-static + no dissipation (no friction, no finite-ΔT heat flow).
adiabatic vs isothermal Adiabatic: q=0 and T usually changes. Isothermal: T constant and usually q=0.
free expansion vs reversible adiabatic expansion Both have q=0. Free: w=0 and ΔT=0 (perfect gas), ΔS>0. Reversible adiabatic: w<0, the gas cools, ΔS=0.
CV​ vs Cp​ CV​=(∂U/∂T)V​ and Cp​=(∂H/∂T)p​; Cp​>CV​.
ΔU vs ΔH qV​=ΔU (bomb calorimeter); qp​=ΔH; they differ by Δ(pV)=Δng​RT.
πT​ vs μ πT​ is (∂U/∂V)T​ (Joule experiment). μ is (∂T/∂p)H​ (Joule–Thomson).
efficiency vs COP Efficiency is W/QH​ for an engine and is always below 1. COP is heat moved per unit work for a fridge or heat pump and can exceed 1.
Kelvin–Planck vs Clausius One is about converting heat to work; the other is about the direction of heat flow. They are equivalent.
microstate vs macrostate Microscopic arrangement vs observed state. W counts the microstates in one macrostate.
thermodynamic vs statistical entropy dS=dqrev​/T (macroscopic, gives changes) vs S=klnW (microscopic, gives absolute values). They agree, e.g. both give nRln2 for doubling V.
real vs Carnot efficiency Real-engine η is always below ηCarnot​, which depends only on the two temperatures.
