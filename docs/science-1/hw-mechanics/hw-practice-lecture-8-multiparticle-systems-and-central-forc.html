Problem 1 [HW practice · MT E8.2]

A particle moves in the logarithmic spiral orbit r=keαθ under an attractive inverse-cube force (Example 8.1). Determine r(t) and θ(t).

Solution

Solution. Angular momentum is conserved for a central force: ℓ=mr2θ˙. With r=keαθ:

θ˙=mr2ℓ​=mk2ℓ​e−2αθ ⇒ e2αθdθ=mk2ℓ​dt

Integrate both sides:

2αe2αθ​=mk2ℓ​t+C′ ⇒ e2αθ=mk22αℓ​t+C

with C=2αC′. Take the logarithm:

θ(t)=2α1​ln(mk22αℓ​t+C)​

and substituting back into r=keαθ:

r(t)=k(mk22αℓ​t+C)1/2​

The constants C and ℓ come from the initial conditions r(0),θ(0) and θ˙(0). The radius grows (or shrinks, for α<0) as t​, and θ grows logarithmically: the particle spirals out with ever slower angular speed.

Problem 2 [HW practice · MT E9.1]

Find the centre of mass of a solid hemisphere of constant density.

Solution

Solution. Let the density be ρ, the radius a, and place the origin at the centre of the flat face with the z axis through the pole. By symmetry X=Y=0. Slice the hemisphere into discs of thickness dz at height z with radius a2−z2​:

dm=ρπ(a2−z2)dz,M=ρπ∫0a​(a2−z2)dz=ρπ(a3−3a3​)=32πρa3​
Z=M1​∫zdm=Mρπ​∫0a​z(a2−z2)dz=Mρπ​(2a4​−4a4​)=2πρa3/3ρπa4/4​

so the centre of mass lies on the axis, 3a/8 above the centre of the flat face.

Problem 3 [HW practice · MT E9.3]

A light string of length a with masses m1​ and m2​ at its ends is whirled around by hand above the head and released. Describe the subsequent motion and find the tension in the string after release.

Solution

Solution. After release the only external forces are the gravitational forces on m1​ and m2​, whose resultant acts at the centre of mass. Hence:

  1. The centre of mass moves in a parabola, exactly like a single projectile of mass m1​+m2​ launched with the CM velocity at release.
  2. About the centre of mass there is no external torque (gravity acts at the CM), so the angular momentum about the CM is conserved: the pair keeps rotating with the same angular velocity ω as at release, with the string taut.

The CM is a distance b=m1​+m2​m1​​a from m2​ (and a−b=m1​+m2​m2​​a from m1​). The tension supplies the centripetal force for either mass; for m2​, moving in a circle of radius b at angular speed ω:

T=m2​bω2=m1​+m2​m1​m2​​aω2​

(the same from m1​: m1​(a−b)ω2). The combination μ=m1​m2​/(m1​+m2​) is the reduced mass. The tension is independent of gravity, since both masses are in free fall.

Problem 4 [HW practice · MT E9.10]

A rope of mass per unit length ρ and length a is suspended just above a table. It is released from rest at the top. Find the force on the table after the rope has fallen a distance x.

Solution

Solution. The force on the table has two parts: the weight of the rope already lying on the table, and the impulsive force from the rope that is still arriving.

Weight: the length on the table is x, so Fg​=ρxg.

Impulse: each piece of rope falls freely through a distance x before landing, so it arrives with speed v2=2gx. In time dt a mass dm=ρvdt is stopped, delivering momentum dp=vdm=ρv2dt, so

Fimpulse​=ρv2=2ρgx

Total force on the table:

F=ρxg+2ρxg=3ρxg​

which equals the weight of a length 3x of rope. At the instant the last of the rope hits (x=a) the force is 3ρag=3Mg, three times the weight of the whole rope, then drops suddenly to Mg.

Problem 5 [HW practice · MT 9-15]

A smooth rope is placed above a hole in a table (Figure 9-D). One end of the rope falls through the hole at t=0, pulling steadily on the remainder of the rope. Find the velocity and acceleration of the rope as a function of the distance x to the end of the rope. Ignore all friction. The total length of the rope is L.

Solution

Solution. The rope is a loose pile (heap) on the table, at rest, and only the part hanging through the hole is moving. Let ρ be the mass per unit length. The hanging part has mass ρx and is the only part with momentum p=ρxv. The only external force is its weight ρxg (the table does not push on the heap vertically in a way that matters, and friction is neglected), so

dtd​(ρxv)=ρxg ⇒ v2+xv˙=gx

Use v˙=vdv/dx and multiply by x:

xv2+x2vdxdv​=gx2 ⇒ 21​dxd​(x2v2)=gx2
x2v2=32​gx3+C,C=0 (the motion starts at x=0)
v=32gx​​​

The acceleration:

a=dtdv​=vdxdv​=21​dxd(v2)​=3g​​

The acceleration is constant, g/3, valid for 0≤x≤L (reaching v=2gL/3​ when the last of the rope leaves the hole). Energy is not conserved: the kinetic energy at length x is 21​ρxv2=31​ρgx2 while the potential energy lost is 21​ρgx2, so one third of the energy released (61​ρgx2) is dissipated as the rope is jerked into motion from the heap.
